# HS-LS1-6 Unit: The Giant Pumpkin Mystery

Grade 9 Living Earth (Biology) lessons for **HS-LS1-6**: *Construct and revise an explanation based on evidence for how carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen from sugar molecules may combine with other elements to form amino acids and/or other large carbon-based molecules.*

Seven 45-minute lessons. They lead into the practice test, review, and summative test in [`assessments/HS-LS1-6`](../../assessments/HS-LS1-6/README.md).

**Anchoring phenomenon:** A giant pumpkin seed weighs less than a gram. About four months later the pumpkin can weigh more than 1,200 kg. Where did all that matter come from?

**Driving question:** How does a plant build its body, and what is it built from?

## How the story builds

1. **Day 1:** The pumpkin is mostly carbon and oxygen, and it has almost none of the silicon and aluminum that make up most of soil. So the soil can't be the main source.
2. **Day 2:** Every molecule of life is made of atoms, and atoms don't change into other elements. Sugars and fats contain only C, H, and O. Amino acids also have N. Some molecules also need P or Mg.
3. **Day 3:** Plants build sugar from CO<sub>2</sub> and water. Light supplies the energy, not the atoms.
4. **Day 4:** Atoms from sugar are rearranged and combined with nitrogen from the soil to build amino acids. No atoms are gained or lost.
5. **Day 5:** Amino acids (monomers) link in a specific order to form proteins (polymers). When animals eat, they break food molecules down and rebuild the atoms into *their own* molecules.
6. **Day 6:** Real data back up each part of the model: tracers, nutrient experiments, fungi, and extra-CO<sub>2</sub> experiments.
7. **Day 7:** Students put it all together and explain the pumpkin in writing, using the same rubric as the test.

## Three-dimensional learning

- **Science and engineering practice:** Constructing explanations and designing solutions. Students build an explanation from evidence on Days 1 through 6, revise it on Day 7, and use models (beads, paper clips) to show how atoms rearrange.
- **Disciplinary core idea:** LS1.C Organization for Matter and Energy Flow in Organisms. The sugar molecules formed contain carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen. Their hydrocarbon backbones are used to make amino acids and other carbon-based molecules that can be assembled into larger molecules such as proteins or DNA.
- **Crosscutting concept:** Energy and Matter. Changes of energy and matter in a system can be described as flows into, out of, and within that system. Matter is conserved because atoms are conserved.
- **Assessment boundary:** No chemical reaction details and no identification of macromolecule structures. The bead and paper-clip models count atoms and show linking without teaching reaction mechanisms or structural formulas. Students aren't expected to memorize any formula.

## Using the handouts

- **Print:** open it in a browser and print.
- **Canvas Page:** open the page's HTML editor and paste everything between `<body>` and `</body>`. Or create all seven pages at once with the uploader: `CANVAS_TOKEN=... python3 assessments/HS-LS1-6/canvas_upload.py https://kernhigh.instructure.com <course id> --only lessons`. The pages are created unpublished and named "HS-LS1-6 Lesson 1: ..." through "HS-LS1-6 Lesson 7: ...".
