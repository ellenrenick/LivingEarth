Carbon dioxide and water supply the atoms. Light supplies the energy to rearrange them.

Part 2: Build sugar with beads

Bead key: black = carbon (C), white = hydrogen (H), red = oxygen (O). Each pipe cleaner is one molecule.

Round 1

  1. Build 6 CO2 molecules (1 black + 2 red each) and 6 H2O molecules (2 white + 1 red each). Put them in the “Air and water” cup.
  2. Count your beads and fill in the “Before” row.
  3. Place a LIGHT ENERGY card on your desk. You may only rebuild molecules while the card is out.
  4. Take apart the CO2 and water. Rebuild the beads into 1 glucose (C6H12O6) and 6 O2 molecules (2 red each).
  5. Count your beads again and fill in the “After” row.
Black (C)White (H)Red (O)Total beads
Before (6 CO2 + 6 H2O)
After (1 glucose + 6 O2)

Round 2: A real leaf does this millions of times every second. Build a second glucose the same way. Then take your O2 molecules apart and return those red beads. Put both glucose models in your labeled bag. Save them for tomorrow!

  1. Compare the Before and After rows. What do you notice? What does this show about atoms?
  2. Where did the carbon atoms in your glucose come from? The hydrogen atoms?
  3. What happened to the extra oxygen atoms?
  4. Did the LIGHT ENERGY card turn into any beads? What does the card represent?
  5. Describe one way the bead model is different from what really happens in a leaf.
