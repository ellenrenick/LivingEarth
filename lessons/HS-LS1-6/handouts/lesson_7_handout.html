Part 3: Explain it to the grower

A pumpkin grower sends you these records from this year's giant pumpkin:

RecordValue
Mass of the seedabout 0.5 g
Mass of the pumpkin at harvest1,100 kg
Water in the pumpkinabout 90% of its mass
Dry matter in the pumpkinabout 110 kg
Carbon in the dry matterabout 45% (about 50 kg of carbon)
Nitrogen in the dry matterabout 3% (about 3.3 kg of nitrogen)
Change in soil level in the pumpkin patchtoo small to measure
Fertilizer used“10-10-10 Plant Food”: contains nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium; no sugar and no carbon
Growing conditionssunny summer, watered every day

The grower asks:

“I always figured my pumpkin was built out of the soil and the plant food I gave it. But the soil hardly changed, and the fertilizer doesn't even have carbon in it. So what is my pumpkin actually made of, and how did it get built?”

Write a scientific explanation for the grower. Use this checklist:

✓My explanation…
Claim: says what the pumpkin is made of and where most of its matter came from
Evidence: uses at least two pieces of the grower's data
Evidence: uses at least one piece of evidence from class (element data, bead models, tracer, nitrogen, fungi, or extra CO2)
Reasoning: explains how the leaves build sugar and what supplies the atoms and the energy
Reasoning: explains how the carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen from sugar combine with other elements (like nitrogen) to build amino acids, proteins, and other molecules
Correct the grower: explains what the soil and the “plant food” really do
Energy vs. matter: says what the sunlight did, and whether it became part of the pumpkin













Sentence frames (optional):

  • Your pumpkin is made mostly from ____, not ____.
  • The data show that ____, and in class we found that ____.
  • First, the leaves ____. Sugar contains only ____.
  • Then the plant rearranges ____ and combines them with ____ to build ____.
  • The fertilizer isn't really food because ____. It supplies ____.
  • Sunlight supplied ____, but it didn't become part of the pumpkin because ____.

Part 4: Peer scoring

Swap with a partner. Check off each item on their checklist that's present. Then score their explanation.

ScoreWhat it looks like

Correctly explains the role of the soil, fertilizer, and sunlight.

Before the practice test: Can you…

  • explain where the carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen in sugar come from?
  • explain why sunlight is energy and not matter?
  • use a table of atoms to decide whether a molecule needs an element that sugar doesn't have?
  • explain why a plant with lots of sugar but no nitrogen stays small?
  • trace a carbon atom from the air, into a plant, and into an animal that eats it?
  • use data to revise an explanation?
