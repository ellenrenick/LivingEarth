Bead key: black = carbon (C), white = hydrogen (H), red = oxygen (O), blue = nitrogen (N)

Your cups:

  • Glucose bag: the sugar your leaf made yesterday (atoms from air and water)
  • Soil cup: nitrogen atoms that the roots took in from the soil
  • Recycle tray: atoms you don't use. They don't disappear!

Rules: You can't make new beads. You can't change a bead's color. Unused beads go in the recycle tray, not the trash.

Part 2: Build amino acids

  1. Count the beads in your 2 glucose models. Fill in the “Start” row.
  2. Take apart both glucose models.
  3. Build valine on one pipe cleaner. Take blue beads from the soil cup only when you need them.
  4. Build threonine on another pipe cleaner.
  5. Put all unused beads in the recycle tray.
  6. Count everything and finish the table.

Part 3: Make sense of the model

  1. Which bead colors in your amino acids came from the glucose? Which came from the soil cup?
  2. Why couldn't you build any amino acid with only the beads from glucose?
  3. Predict: A pumpkin gets plenty of light, water, and CO2, but its soil has almost no nitrogen. What could it still make? What would it have trouble making? What would happen to its sugar?
  4. Compare “Total available” to “Total at end.” What does this show about atoms when molecules are built?
  5. Describe one way this model is different from what really happens in a plant cell.

Sentence frames (optional):

  • The carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen in valine came from ____. The nitrogen came from ____.
  • The plant needs nitrogen from the soil because ____.
  • The totals were the same because atoms are ____, not ____.

Exit ticket

A bag of garden fertilizer says “Plant Food!” on the front. The ingredients list nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium. It has no sugar.

Is fertilizer really “food” for a plant? Explain what fertilizer supplies and how the plant uses it along with the sugar it makes.
