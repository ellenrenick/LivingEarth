# Unit 3.1: Age of the Earth (HS-ESS1-5, supporting HS-ESS1-6)

Living Earth (Grade 9 Biology), Semester 1. 8 days, 45-minute periods.
Owner: Ellen. Built from the Earth Science 11 Unit 4 ("Clues in the Crust") blueprint, with biology woven in and the Earth science kept as the backbone.

**Targets source:** the team workbook *Renick Living Earth Standards and Learning Targets (26-27)*, Targets tab (8 targets: ESS1-5.1 to 5.5 priority, ESS1-6.1 to 6.3 supporting). The workbook's rule: **priority targets are assessed on the CSA; supporting targets are taught and practiced, never on the CSA.** Origin is "GATE-proposed" (for team review).

## What changed from the first version of this unit

The first version had five targets that I wrote (continental drift evidence, seafloor spreading, boundaries and subduction, radiometric dating, and a CER). After the updated workbook, the unit follows the workbook targets exactly:

- Three new targets were added: ESS1-5.5 (evaluate how well plate tectonics explains the ages), ESS1-6.2, and ESS1-6.3.
- ESS1-5.3 now includes how continents grow outward from an ancient core, and ESS1-5.2 includes the North American crust-age pattern.
- ESS1-5.4 is now "evaluate the evidence" (rock ages, magnetic stripes, measured motion), not the CER. The fossil-puzzle evidence moved into Day 5 as one line of evidence.
- The CSA dropped its ESS1-6.1 items to follow the tier rule. It has five targets, not six.
- The magnetic-stripe model moved to Day 5, boundary plotting moved to Day 1, and the half-life lab moved to Day 3 with the impact-crater record.

## Where the biology lives (and where it stops)

| Thread | Day | The Earth science question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hydrothermal vent communities and chemosynthesis | Enrichment (Option 1) | What lives on brand-new crust? |
| Seafloor sediment made of plankton shells, thicker on older crust | 2, 6 | Does sediment thickness agree with the crust-age pattern, and does it rule out "erosion" as the cause of young ocean floor? |
| Subduction carries ocean sediment and carbonate shells into the mantle; arc volcanoes return CO2 | 4 | Where does ocean floor go, and what happens to what is on it? (Links back to Unit 1.2 Carbon.) |
| Fossils on separated continents (Glossopteris, Mesosaurus, Lystrosaurus) | 5 | Is matching life evidence that continents were once joined? |
| Carbon-14 versus uranium-lead | 3 | Which "clock" can date which sample? |
| Life's oldest fossils are in continental rock | 3, 6 | What does the young seafloor cost us? |

Kept out on purpose: evolution mechanisms, speciation, and extinction causes. Those belong to Units 3.2 and 3.3.

| Min | What happens |

### Day 2 · Reading Rock Ages (ESS1-5.2; CFA ESS1-5.1 opens the day)

| Min | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0–8 | **CFA ESS1-5.1** |
| 8–12 | Warm-up prediction: ridge sample versus Brazil sample. |

| 33–45 | Continents have a pattern too: North American crust is oldest in the Canadian Shield core (about 2.7 billion years) and younger outward. CER log. |

### Day 4 · Why the Ocean Is Young and the Continents Old (ESS1-5.3; no CFA opens the day)

| Min | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0–2 | Warm-up. |
| 2–12 | Density and subduction (ocean crust 3.0, continental crust 2.7 g/cm³). |
| 12–18 | The life of ocean crust: 3,000 km at 25 km per million years is 120 million years. |
| 18–28 | How continents grow: an ancient core with arcs and crust added at the edges, like tree rings. |
| 28–32 | Biology link: plankton-shell carbon rides the plate down. |
| 32–45 | SketchNotes: ridge, trench, growing continent. |

**Teacher note:** Ocean *crust* alone is slightly less dense than the mantle. It is the old, cold ocean *plate* that is denser than the hot mantle below and sinks. Keep comparisons to plate against plate.

| Day | Evidence | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |

## CER rubric (4 points)

| Score | Description |
| --- | --- |
| 4 | Part A: complete, accurate claim about how well plate tectonics explains the ages; at least two specific pieces of evidence including both ocean and continental crust; reasoning that connects the evidence to the process (made at ridges, destroyed at subduction zones, continents too buoyant to sink and growing at their edges); a limit or counterclaim. Part B: three or more events in order with ages and evidence, and the missing record explained. |
| 3 | Accurate claim; two specific pieces of evidence; reasoning present but incomplete or generic. |
| 2 | Claim and one piece of evidence, or two vague pieces; reasoning missing or restates the evidence. |
| 1 | Claim only, or the claim and evidence are partly incorrect. |
| 0 | Blank or off-task. |

## Unit 3.1 CSA: Age of the Earth (HS-ESS1-5)

About 45 minutes. 16 questions on a 4-point scale, built as a New Quiz in the same layout as the Unit 1.3 CSA. Questions 12 and 16 are teacher-graded written responses (4-point rubrics); the other 14 are auto-graded.

| Target | Questions | Item types |

| ESS1-5.1 | Q1–Q3 | Path of ocean crust, matching boundary types with examples, plates carry continental and oceanic crust (true/false) |

## Intervention and enrichment

**Intervention (CFA score below 3 of 4, which is fewer than 4 of 5 correct):** a Mastery Path review assignment opens for that target only. Each review is a short study page with worked "Try it" checks (answers hidden under a click) and 3 written questions (pass/fail, 4 points). Review answers are in the teacher key.

**Enrichment (if the score is 3 or 4):** Choice board; pick one.
1. **Life at the ridge:** vent food web, chemosynthesis versus photosynthesis, spreading-rate arithmetic.
2. **The missing record:** a scaled timeline and why the fossil record for the first 3 billion years is nearly all from continental rock.
3. **Iceland:** a divergent boundary on land.
4. **A planet without plate tectonics:** Mars.

## Supports

- Pre-teach vocabulary with pictures on Day 1.
- Sentence frames on all argument writing.
- Pair stronger and weaker readers; pre-cut strips and continent outlines.
- Allow students to point to evidence cards rather than write, then say the sentence orally first.
