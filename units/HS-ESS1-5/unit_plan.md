# Unit 3.1: Age of the Earth (HS-ESS1-5, supporting HS-ESS1-6)

Living Earth (Grade 9 Biology), Semester 1. 8 days, 45-minute periods.
Owner: Ellen. Built from the Earth Science 11 Unit 4 ("Clues in the Crust") blueprint, with biology woven in and the Earth science kept as the backbone.

## Where the biology lives (and where it stops)

Each thread below is tied to a rock-age claim so it never drifts into a separate biology unit.

| Thread | Day | The Earth science question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fossils on separated continents (Glossopteris, Mesosaurus, Lystrosaurus, Cynognathus) | 2 | Were the continents once joined? |
| Hydrothermal vent communities exist only on young crust at ridges, powered by chemosynthesis | 3 | What is happening at the ridge today, and how long does any one patch of crust last? |
| Seafloor sediment is made of microscopic plankton shells and gets thicker away from the ridge | 3 | Does sediment thickness agree with the crust-age pattern? |
| Subduction carries ocean sediment, including carbonate shells, into the mantle, and arc volcanoes return CO2 to the air | 4 | Where does ocean floor go, and what happens to what is on it? (Links back to Unit 1.2 Carbon.) |
| Carbon-14 versus uranium-lead: only some "clocks" can date some materials | 5 | Which method can date which sample? |
| Life's oldest fossils are in continental rock; no ocean floor holds the first 3 billion years of life | 5, 6 | What does the young seafloor cost us, and why is the fossil record mostly a continental record? |

Kept out on purpose: evolution mechanisms, speciation, and extinction causes. Those belong to Units 3.2 and 3.3.

## Day-by-day plan

Each Canvas day follows the Unit 1.3 pattern: SubHeader, student Page, then the CFA and its Mastery Path review. Every student page opens with the day's learning target, a notebook setup, and a unit CER evidence-log row.

### Day 1 · Where Did the Old Ocean Floor Go? (launch)

**Targets:** Launch for all. Pre-teach vocabulary.
**Phenomenon:** A world map of seafloor age (colored by age, 0 to about 200 million years) beside a map of continental rock ages (oldest about 4 billion years).

| Min | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Warm-up: "If Earth is 4.5 billion years old, how old do you think the ocean floor is? The continents?" Students record a number for each, with a reason. |
| 5–15 | Seafloor age map. Notice and wonder in pairs: where is the young rock, where is the old, and what shape does the pattern make? |
| 15–22 | Reveal the other half of the puzzle: continental rocks up to about 4 billion years old, and the oldest fossils (about 3.5 billion years old) in continental rock. Add the line: "The oldest ocean floor is about 200 million years old." |
| 22–30 | Name the mystery. Students write an initial claim for the unit CER: "Why is the seafloor so much younger than the continents?" and draw an initial model. |
| 30–40 | Vocabulary pre-teach with pictures. Start the Frayer cards. Two words per pair; share out. |
| 40–45 | Exit ticket: one prediction for what evidence would test their claim. |

**Materials:** Seafloor age map (NOAA NCEI seafloor age grid or any labeled copy), continental rock age map or table, Frayer template.
**Teaching notes:** Do not explain plate tectonics yet. Collect wrong ideas (for example "the ocean floor gets eroded away" or "the ocean is new") for revisiting on Day 6.
**Assessment:** No CFA. Initial claim is the CER baseline.

### Day 2 · The Fossil Puzzle (ESS1-5.1)

| Min | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0–4 | Warm-up: a fossil of a small freshwater reptile (*Mesosaurus*) is found in both Brazil and South Africa. Can it swim across the Atlantic? Why or why not? |
| 4–12 | Wegener's idea (1912): the continents were joined. Fit the continents using the continental shelf edge, not the coastline. |
| 12–35 | **Fossil puzzle map lab:** pairs cut out the southern continents and tape them into a fit, then plot four fossil ranges on the fitted map (see below). Then add matching rock layers, a matching mountain belt (Appalachian–Caledonian), and glacial scratch directions on the southern continents. |
| 35–39 | Wegener's weakness: he had no mechanism. Why did scientists doubt him? |
| 39–45 | CFA ESS1-5.1. Update the CER evidence log. |

**Fossil ranges to plot (all consistent with a joined Pangaea):**

| Organism | Type | Found in | Age |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Glossopteris* | seed fern | South America, Africa, India, Australia, Antarctica | Permian, about 250–300 million years |
| *Mesosaurus* | freshwater reptile | South America, southern Africa | Early Permian, about 280 million years |
| *Lystrosaurus* | land reptile relative | Africa, India, Antarctica | Early Triassic, about 250 million years |
| *Cynognathus* | land reptile relative | South America, southern Africa | Triassic, about 240 million years |

**Biology reasoning prompt:** The seeds of *Glossopteris* were large and heavy and would not drift across an ocean. The animals could not swim across salt water. Which explanation fits better: "they crossed a gap" or "there was no gap"?

**CFA ESS1-5.1:** 5 auto-graded questions, built as a New Quiz. Questions and answers are in `assessments/HS-ESS1-5/HS-ESS1-5_teacher_key.md`.

### Day 3 · Making New Crust (ESS1-5.2)

| Min | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Warm-up: a sample of seafloor rock is pulled up at the ridge, and another near the coast of Brazil. Predict which is older and why. |
| 5–12 | Data: age versus distance from the Mid-Atlantic Ridge (table below). Graph it. Find the rate. |
| 12–30 | **Paper magnetic-stripe model:** a strip of paper with alternating stripes is fed through a slot; students pull it from the middle outward, creating mirror-image bands, and relate the bands to magnetic reversals. |
| 30–36 | Life on new crust: hydrothermal vents (found in 1977 at the Galápagos Rift). Chemosynthesis, not photosynthesis, supports the community. A vent site lasts decades, and the crust beneath it moves away from the ridge. |
| 36–40 | Sediment thickness versus distance. Plankton shells settle slowly, so older crust has had more time to collect sediment. |
| 40–45 | CFA ESS1-5.2. Update the CER evidence log. |

**Data for graphing (simplified from a spreading rate of about 2.5 cm/yr; sediment values are illustrative for classroom use, replace with measured values from NOAA or DSDP/IODP if you want a real data set):**

| Distance from ridge (km) | Crust age (million years) | Sediment thickness (m) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | about 0 |
| 500 | 20 | about 50 |
| 1,000 | 40 | about 150 |
| 1,500 | 60 | about 250 |
| 2,000 | 80 | about 350 |
| 2,500 | 100 | about 450 |
| 3,000 | 120 | about 550 |

**Rate check:** 500 km in 20 million years = 25 km per million years = **2.5 cm per year**, about the speed fingernails grow.

**CFA ESS1-5.2:** 5 auto-graded questions, built as a New Quiz. Questions and answers are in `assessments/HS-ESS1-5/HS-ESS1-5_teacher_key.md`.

### Day 4 · Where Crust Goes, and Where It Stays (ESS1-5.3, SketchNotes)

| Min | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0–4 | Warm-up: if new crust is made at ridges and Earth is not getting bigger, where does the old crust go? |
| 4–14 | Quake and volcano map: students plot a small set of earthquake and volcano points (Ring of Fire, Mid-Atlantic Ridge, San Andreas) and describe the pattern. Name the three boundary types and match each to its landforms. |
| 14–24 | Density demo and discussion: ocean crust (basalt, about 3.0 g/cm³) versus continental crust (granite, about 2.7 g/cm³). When they collide, which plate sinks, and why? Why does an ocean plate get denser as it ages? (It cools as it moves away from the ridge.) |
| 24–28 | **Biology link:** the ocean floor carries a layer of sediment, including carbonate shells from dead plankton, down with it. Arc volcanoes release CO2. Connect to Unit 1.2. |
| 28–40 | SketchNotes: half page of notes, one page of sensemaking (a labeled cross-section of a subduction zone with the trench, volcanic arc, and sinking plate). Finish the Frayer cards as homework if needed. |
| 40–45 | Update the CER evidence log. Finish SketchNotes and Frayer cards as homework. CFA ESS1-5.3 is taken at the start of Day 5, so Day 4 does not run over. |

**Why continents survive (the key idea):** Continental crust is thick and less dense (about 2.7 g/cm³), so it is buoyant and rarely pulled down. Old ocean plates are cold and dense (the crust is about 3.0 g/cm³, and the cold mantle rock beneath it adds to the pull), so they can sink. Some continental rock is eroded and recycled, but most of it stays at the surface for billions of years.

**Teacher note:** Ocean *crust* alone is slightly less dense than the mantle. It is the old, cold ocean *plate* (crust plus the rock beneath it) that is denser than the hot mantle below and sinks. Keep student comparisons to plate against plate, as the CFA and CSA do.

**CFA ESS1-5.3:** 5 auto-graded questions, built as a New Quiz. Questions and answers are in `assessments/HS-ESS1-5/HS-ESS1-5_teacher_key.md`.

### Day 5 · Reading Deep Time (ESS1-6.1; CFA ESS1-5.3 opens the day)

| Min | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0–8 | **CFA ESS1-5.3** (a spaced check on Day 4; it replaces the warm-up). |
| 8–12 | Hook: the oldest ocean floor is about 200 million years old, but scientists say Earth is 4.5 billion years old. How could anyone know that? Radiometric dating: unstable atoms decay at a steady rate. Half-life defined. |
| 12–30 | **Half-life lab:** shake-and-count with coins or candies. Remove "decayed" pieces each round, graph the remaining atoms versus rounds, and find that half remain after each half-life. |
| 30–37 | Which clock for which sample? Students match samples to methods (table below). |
| 37–45 | The ESS1-6 payoff: Earth's oldest known minerals are about 4.4 billion years (zircons), the oldest known rocks about 4.0 billion years, and meteorites about 4.56 billion. Plate tectonics and erosion recycled the earliest rocks, but meteorites and the Moon kept their record. Update the CER evidence log. CFA ESS1-6.1 is taken at the start of Day 6. |

**Which clock?**

| Method | Parent → daughter | Half-life | Useful for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carbon-14 | C-14 → N-14 | 5,730 years | Once-living material (wood, bone, shell) younger than about 50,000 years |
| Uranium-235 | U-235 → Pb-207 | about 704 million years | Zircon crystals in igneous rock and meteorites |
| Uranium-238 | U-238 → Pb-206 | about 4.47 billion years | Zircon crystals in igneous rock and meteorites |

**Teaching note:** Zircon crystals take uranium into their structure when they form but exclude lead, so any lead they contain came from decay. That is why they work as clocks.

**Life's oldest evidence (read-aloud or short text):** The oldest widely accepted fossils are stromatolites about 3.5 billion years old, preserved in continental rock in Western Australia. No ocean floor survives from that time, so the fossil record of early life comes from continental rock.

**CFA ESS1-6.1:** 5 auto-graded questions, built as a New Quiz. Questions and answers are in `assessments/HS-ESS1-5/HS-ESS1-5_teacher_key.md`.

### Day 6 · Build the Argument (ESS1-5.4)

| Min | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0–8 | **CFA ESS1-6.1** (a spaced check on Day 5; it replaces the warm-up). |
| 8–13 | Review the evidence log. Students sort evidence into three buckets: the seafloor is young (Days 3 and 4), the continents are old (Days 2 and 5), and the record is missing (Days 4 and 5). Return to the Day 1 claim: what would you change? |
| 13–27 | Draft the CER using sentence frames (below). |
| 27–38 | Peer feedback with a checklist: Is the claim a complete answer? Is each piece of evidence specific (a number or a named pattern)? Does the reasoning connect the evidence to a process? |
| 38–45 | **CFA ESS1-5.4** (checks what makes a strong claim, evidence, and reasoning). |

**Sentence frames**
- **Claim:** The ocean floor is much younger than the continents because ______.
- **Evidence 1 (pattern):** The seafloor age map shows ______.
- **Evidence 2 (data):** At a rate of about ______, rock ______ km from the ridge is about ______ million years old.
- **Evidence 3 (continents):** Continental rocks as old as ______ exist because ______.
- **Reasoning:** Because ocean crust is ______ than continental crust, it ______ at subduction zones, so ______.
- **Counterclaim:** Someone might say the ocean floor is young because ______. This does not fit the evidence because ______.

**CFA ESS1-5.4:** 5 auto-graded questions on choosing a complete claim, telling evidence from reasoning, picking the strongest evidence, and answering a counterclaim. The written CER is scored with the rubric below and also feeds the CSA.

## Unit CER evidence log

One row per day. Columns: Day, evidence (specific number, pattern, or fossil), what it shows about the question "Why is the seafloor so much younger than the continents?"

Example rows to model on Day 1:
| Day | Evidence | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | Crust 1,000 km from the ridge is about 40 million years old; crust 3,000 km away is about 120 million. | Crust gets older with distance from the ridge, so it is made at the ridge. |

## CER rubric (4 points)

| Score | Description |
| --- | --- |
| 4 | Complete, accurate claim; at least two specific pieces of evidence from different lines (for example the age pattern and the density difference, or the age pattern and the oldest continental rock); reasoning that connects the evidence to the process (made at ridges, destroyed at subduction zones, continents too buoyant to sink); addresses a counterclaim or the missing-record idea. |
| 3 | Accurate claim; two specific pieces of evidence; reasoning present but incomplete or generic. |
| 2 | Claim and one piece of evidence, or two vague pieces of evidence; reasoning missing or mostly restates the evidence. |
| 1 | Claim only, or the claim and evidence are partly incorrect. |
| 0 | Blank or off-task. |

## Unit 3.1 CSA: Age of the Earth (HS-ESS1-5, HS-ESS1-6)

About 45 minutes. 16 questions on a 4-point scale, built as a New Quiz in the same layout as the Unit 1.3 CSA. Questions 14 and 16 are teacher-graded written responses (4-point rubrics); the other 14 are auto-graded.

| Target | Questions | Item types |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ESS1-5.1 | Q1–Q3 | Fossil evidence (choice), three lines of evidence (select three), Wegener's missing mechanism (true/false) |
| ESS1-5.2 | Q4–Q6 | Spreading-rate calculation from a table, mirror-image magnetic stripes, sediment thickness across three drill sites |
| ESS1-5.3 | Q7–Q9 | Reading a trench and volcano pattern, which plate subducts given densities, why continental crust survives |
| ESS1-5.4 | Q13, Q14 (written), Q15 | Completing the reasoning, the CER, why no early fossils are in ocean-floor rock |
| ESS1-6.1 | Q10–Q12, Q16 (written) | Half-life calculation, matching a sample to a dating method, interpreting zircon and meteorite ages, revising an explanation |

The written CER is **Q14** and the revise-an-explanation is **Q16**.

**Q14 (CER, 4 points):** "A student says, 'The ocean floor is young because the ocean is young.' Use the seafloor age map and the data packet to write a CER that explains why the ocean floor is much younger than the continents."

**Data in the question:** a crust age and distance table (0 to 120 million years across 3,000 km); the oldest ocean floor (about 200 million years); the oldest continental minerals (about 4.4 billion years); ocean crust about 3.0 g/cm³ versus continental crust about 2.7 g/cm³; and ocean plates sinking at trenches. If you want students to also use the seafloor age map, add it to the question as an image.

**Q16 (revise an explanation, 4 points):** A student writes, "Scientists cannot know how old the Earth is because the oldest rock on Earth is only about 4 billion years old." Revise the explanation using what meteorites and plate tectonics tell us. A strong answer states that Earth's earliest rocks were destroyed or recycled, that meteorites formed with the solar system and have changed little, and that their ages (about 4.56 billion years) give the best estimate for Earth.

## Intervention and enrichment

**Intervention (CFA score below 3 of 4, which is fewer than 4 of 5 correct):** a Mastery Path review assignment opens for that target only. Each review is a short study page with worked "Try it" checks (answers hidden under a click) and 3 written questions (pass/fail, 4 points). Review answers are in the teacher key.

**Enrichment (if the score is 3 or 4):** Choice board; pick one.
1. **Life at the ridge:** research how vent animals get energy and why a vent community dies when a vent shuts off. Write a short paragraph linking the vent to the crust age pattern.
2. **The missing record:** design a poster showing why the fossil record for the first 3 billion years of life is nearly all from continental rock.
3. **Iceland:** a divergent boundary on land. Use a photo or a map to describe what is forming and how fast.
4. **Listening to Mars (extension):** Mars has no plate tectonics. Predict what its crust ages would look like and why craters would last.
