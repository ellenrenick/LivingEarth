# HS-ESS1-5 Review: Answer Key

Use with `HS-ESS1-5_review.html`. Students do it on the review day, after the CFAs and before the CSA. Plan on 30 to 40 minutes. The Vocabulary and Write It sections are for everyone; students pick the other sections by their CFA scores (below 4 of 6).

## Vocabulary

1. tectonic plate
2. mid-ocean ridge
3. subduction zone
4. divergent boundary
5. convergent boundary
6. transform boundary

## Section A: Plates and boundaries

1. Divergent boundary; new crust is made.
2. Convergent boundary (subduction zone); ocean crust is destroyed.
3. Transform boundary; crust is neither made nor destroyed.
4. It is made at mid-ocean ridges and sinks at subduction zones.

## Section B: Reading rock-age data

1. Rock gets older as distance from the ridge increases.
2. New crust forms at the ridge and spreads outward over time.
3. 600 / 30 = 20 km per million years. At 900 km: 900 / 20 = 45 million years.
4. A matching pattern: ages increase with distance on both sides.

## Section C: Why ocean crust is young and continents are old

1. It is continually made at ridges and recycled into the mantle at subduction zones.
2. It is less dense and is not recycled as easily.
3. It grew outward from an ancient core as plate interactions added newer crust at the edges.

## Section D: How strong is the evidence?

1. GPS: current. Seafloor ages: past. Fossils: past.
2. They are independent and all point to the same conclusion.
3. Example: GPS covers only a few decades; fossils alone could be explained by animals crossing a land bridge.

## Section E: Judging the theory

1. Yes. New crust forms at the ridge and spreads both ways.
2. Ocean crust is recycled at subduction zones, so older ocean floor has been destroyed.
3. Example: how the first continental crust formed, or how plate motion began.
4. Strong: it explains very different ages with one set of processes, and it matches many independent measurements.

## Write It

**Checklist (what a strong answer includes):**

- Rejects the claim that the continents have not moved
- Uses at least two lines of evidence with specific details
- Says whether each line shows past or current movement
- Judges that the evidence is strong because independent lines agree
- Names a reasonable limit of one line of evidence

**Model answer:** The claim is not supported; the continents have moved in the past and are moving now. Greenland is moving away from Europe at about 2 cm per year, which shows current motion. The seafloor is 0 million years old at the Mid-Atlantic Ridge and 100 million years old 2,000 km away on both sides, which shows new crust formed at the ridge and spread apart over time. A matching mountain belt in North America and Scotland suggests they were once joined. The evidence is strong because three independent kinds agree. One limit is that GPS covers only a short time, so alone it cannot show what happened millions of years ago.
