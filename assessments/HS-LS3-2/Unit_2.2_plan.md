# Unit 2.2: Inherited Instructions (HS-LS3-2) · day-by-day plan

**Anchoring phenomenon:** The Puppy Puzzle. How can puppies from the same parents be similar to their parents, but not exactly the same as them or each other?

**Performance expectation:** Make and defend a claim based on evidence that inheritable genetic variations may result from (1) new genetic combinations through meiosis, (2) viable errors occurring during replication, and/or (3) mutations caused by environmental factors.

**Boundary:** No phases of meiosis; no biochemical mechanism of specific steps.

## Learning targets

| Target | Tier | DOK | I can |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LS3-2.1 | Priority | 1 | I can name three ways new genetic variation arises. |
| LS3-2.2 | Priority | 2 | I can describe how making sex cells and fertilization mix alleles into new combinations. |
| LS3-2.3 | Priority | 2 | I can explain how copying errors and things like UV light change genes, and when that change is inherited. |
| LS3-2.4 | Priority | 3 | I can read data and make a claim, backed by that data, about what caused a genetic variation. |
| LS3-2.5 | Priority | 3 | I can defend my claim against another explanation using the evidence. |

### Day 1 · Mon Nov 9 · The Puppy Puzzle

**Focus question:** How can puppies from the same parents be alike, but not identical?

| Min | Section | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Notebook setup | Students copy slide 2. |
| 5 | What do you notice? What do you wonder? | Show your puppy photos (or the data on the next slide). Two notices and two wonders each; take 3 aloud. Steer toward: alike in some traits, different in others. |
| 5 | Compare the traits | Partners check Puppies B, C, D. Debrief: every puppy matches one parent for each trait, but each puppy has a different mix. If you use your own photos, swap the table for what you see. |
| 8 | The problem and my first model | Do not correct models. Students revise them on Days 2 and 10. |
| 8 | Three possible explanations | About 2 to 3 minutes per box. Idea 3 gets sharper later: some environmental effects change only how an animal looks (not inherited); others, like UV light, can change the DNA itself (a mutation). |
| 4 | Key idea | Key: information; differences. |
| 3 | Why can siblings be different? | Collect. No CFA today: LS3-2.1 is on Day 2, after students have seen the idea of mixing. |

- The puppy photos are yours (the Nov 9 Genetic Variation Introduction Notes doc). The trait table is a stand-in, so the lesson works without photos.
- This lesson is the 57-minute "Why Aren't Offspring Identical to Their Parents" doc, trimmed to about 33 minutes: notice and wonder and compare traits are shortened, the problem and first model are combined, and the three sketch boxes are quick.
- No CFA today. The three ideas are the three sources of variation in LS3-2.1 (new combinations, copying errors, and mutations from the environment). Do not name them yet.

| Min | Section | What happens |
