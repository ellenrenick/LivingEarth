## Unit 2.2 CFA LS3-2.1: Three sources of variation

**Target:** I can name three ways new genetic variation arises.

**Question 1.** Which list names three ways new genetic variation can arise?

- A. Eating new foods, exercising more often, and learning new skills over many years
- B. New combinations of alleles, copying errors in DNA, and mutations from the environment
- C. Photosynthesis, cellular respiration, and the movement of carbon through all ecosystems
- D. Changes in the weather, changes in the soil, and changes in how much food there is

**Question 2.** What is a mutation?

- A. A new combination of alleles made when an egg and a sperm join
- B. A protein that copies the DNA before a cell divides into two cells
- C. A trait that shows when an offspring has two recessive alleles
- D. A change in the order of the bases in the DNA of a cell

**Question 3.** True or false: UV light and some chemicals can cause mutations by damaging DNA.

**Question 4.** Two parents each have the alleles B and b. Which source of variation best explains why their puppies can be BB, Bb, or bb?

- A. New combinations of alleles when sex cells join at fertilization
- B. Copying errors that happen in every cell of the developing puppy
- C. Mutations caused by UV light shining on the puppies after birth
- D. The puppies' food and water changing their DNA as they grow

**Question 5.** Select the **TWO** sources of new genetic variation.

- A. Errors made when DNA is copied before a cell divides
- B. Mutations caused by UV light or chemicals
- C. Exercise and a healthy diet during a puppy's whole life
- D. Learning new tricks from other dogs in the kennel

**Answer key**

| Question | Answer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | B | The three sources are new combinations of alleles (sex cells and fertilization), errors when DNA is copied, and mutations caused by the environment. |
| 2 | D | A mutation is a change in the DNA sequence. |
| 3 | True | True. UV light, X-rays, and some chemicals are environmental factors that can damage DNA and cause mutations. |
| 4 | A | Each parent passes on one allele, so the puppies get different combinations of the alleles the parents already have. |
| 5 | A and B | Copying errors and mutations from the environment change the DNA. Exercise and learning do not. |


## Unit 2.2 CFA LS3-2.2: Sex cells and fertilization

**Target:** I can describe how making sex cells and fertilization mix alleles into new combinations.

**Question 1.** How many alleles of one gene does each sex cell (egg or sperm) carry?

- A. Two alleles of each gene, one from each of the parent's own parents
- B. One allele of each gene, chosen at random from the two the parent has
- C. Four alleles of each gene, two from the mother and two from the father
- D. No alleles, because sex cells do not have any genes inside of them

**Question 2.** A parent has the alleles Gg. Which sex cells can this parent make?

- A. Only sex cells that carry both G and g together at once
- B. Only sex cells with the dominant allele, which is the G allele
- C. Only sex cells that carry two copies, either two G or two g
- D. Some sex cells with G and some sex cells with g

**Question 3.** Two Gg parents are crossed.

| | G | g |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G | GG | Gg |
| g | Gg | gg |

How many of the 4 boxes show the genotype gg?

- A. 1 of the 4 boxes
- B. 2 of the 4 boxes
- C. 3 of the 4 boxes
- D. 0 of the 4 boxes

**Question 4.** What happens at fertilization?

- A. A body cell copies all of its DNA, so the offspring gets two copies of the same allele
- B. An egg splits in two, so the offspring gets both of its alleles from its mother only
- C. A sperm and an egg join, so the offspring gets one allele from each parent
- D. A mutation changes the alleles, so the offspring gets alleles that neither parent had

**Question 5.** Two siblings have the same parents. One is BB and the other is Bb. What best explains this?

- A. One sibling's DNA mutated, because siblings can never have different genotypes
- B. Siblings with the same parents always get the same alleles, so a record is wrong
- C. Each sibling got a different combination of alleles from the same two parents
- D. The environment changed one sibling's alleles after that sibling was born

**Answer key**

| Question | Answer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | B | Each sex cell carries one allele of each gene. A child gets one from each parent, so it has two. |
| 2 | D | Each sex cell gets just one of the parent's two alleles, so about half carry G and half carry g. |
| 3 | A | Only one box has a g from the top and a g from the side, so 1 of 4 boxes is gg. |
| 4 | C | Fertilization joins two sex cells. The offspring gets one allele from each parent: a new combination. |
| 5 | C | Sex cells carry one allele at random, so each fertilization can make a different combination. |


## Unit 2.2 CFA LS3-2.3: Copying errors and mutations

**Target:** I can explain how copying errors and things like UV light change genes, and when that change is inherited.

**Question 1.** A cell copies its DNA before it divides, and one letter is copied wrong. What is this?

- A. A new combination of alleles from fertilization
- B. A copying error that can become a mutation
- C. A change that is always caused by the environment
- D. A normal change that never affects the DNA at all

**Question 2.** True or false: A mutation in a person's skin cell can be passed on to their children.

**Question 3.** In an animal, which mutation can be inherited by its offspring?

- A. A mutation in a muscle cell of its leg
- B. A mutation in a skin cell caused by a sunburn
- C. A mutation in a lung cell caused by smoke
- D. A mutation in a cell that makes eggs or sperm

**Question 4.** A lifeguard has many UV-damaged skin cells. Why won't her children inherit these mutations?

- A. The mutations are in skin cells, which do not become eggs or sperm
- B. UV light never changes the DNA of any cell in the whole human body
- C. Mutations are only passed on from fathers to children, never from mothers
- D. Mutations in skin cells are always repaired before they can ever matter

**Question 5.** A one-letter mutation changes one amino acid in a protein. Why might this matter?

- A. The protein is no longer made by any of the cells of the body
- B. Every other gene in the cell changes in the same way at once
- C. A different amino acid can make the protein work differently
- D. The mutation is copied into every cell of the body right away

**Answer key**

| Question | Answer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | B | An error made when DNA is copied that is not fixed becomes a mutation. |
| 2 | False | False. Only mutations in cells that make eggs or sperm can be passed to the next generation. |
| 3 | D | Only changes in cells that become eggs or sperm are inherited. |
| 4 | A | A mutation is inherited only when it is in a cell that makes eggs or sperm. |
| 5 | C | Base order sets amino acid order, and a different amino acid can change how a protein works (Unit 2.1). |


## Unit 2.2 CFA LS3-2.4: Read data, make a claim

**Target:** I can read data and make a claim, backed by that data, about what caused a genetic variation.

**Question 1.** A coat-color gene has the alleles F and f.

| | Alleles for coat color |
| --- | --- |
| Mom | F and f |
| Dad | F and f |
| Puppy | f and f |

Which claim is best supported by the data?

- A. The puppy's alleles came from a mutation, because the puppy differs from both parents
- B. The puppy's alleles came from a new combination, because f was already in both parents
- C. The puppy's alleles came from UV light, because the puppy looks different from its parents
- D. The puppy's alleles came from a copying error, because the puppy has two of the same allele

**Question 2.** A coat-color gene:

| | Allele 1 | Allele 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mom | CAT | CAT |
| Dad | CAT | CGT |
| Puppy | CAT | CCT |

Which claim is best supported by the data?

- A. The CCT allele came from fertilization, because the puppy gets one allele from each parent
- B. The CCT allele came from the dad, because the dad has a CGT allele in his DNA
- C. The CCT allele is not new, because the mom has the CAT allele on both copies
- D. The CCT allele came from a mutation, because neither parent has a CCT allele

**Question 3.** Scientists exposed mice to X-rays.

| Group of mice | X-ray dose | New mutations per 1,000 sperm cells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | none | 3 |
| B | low | 12 |
| C | high | 25 |

Which claim is best supported by the data?

- A. X-rays increased the number of mutations, because the counts rise as the dose rises
- B. X-rays had no effect on mutations, because the group with no X-rays also had mutations
- C. Copying errors caused every mutation, because all three groups had some mutations
- D. The low dose caused more mutations than the high dose, because the low dose came first

**Question 4.** Which new data would best show that a new allele came from an environmental mutagen and not from a copying error?

- A. The total number of puppies in each litter that the parents had over several years
- B. The coat colors of the puppies' grandparents on both sides of the whole family tree
- C. Mutation counts for a group exposed to the mutagen and a group that was not exposed
- D. The number of alleles that each sex cell of the parents carries for the same gene

**Question 5.** Scientists compared mutation counts.

| Group | Exposed to the chemical? | New mutations per 1,000 cells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Yes | 5 |
| 2 | No | 5 |

A student claims the chemical caused mutations. Which statement best evaluates the claim?

- A. The data support the claim, because the exposed group had some mutations in its own cells
- B. The data support the claim, because chemicals always change the DNA of every cell in the body
- C. The data do not support the claim, because both groups had the same number of mutations
- D. The data cannot be used, because there are two groups to compare in the table

**Answer key**

| Question | Answer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | B | Both parents carry f, so the puppy ff is a new pairing of alleles the parents already had. |
| 2 | D | Neither parent has CCT, so it is a new allele: a mutation. |
| 3 | A | The mutation count goes up with the dose. The no-X-ray group shows the baseline from copying errors. |
| 4 | C | Comparing an exposed group with an unexposed group shows whether the exposure changed the number of mutations. |
| 5 | C | If the chemical caused mutations, the exposed group would have more. Both groups had 5. |


## Unit 2.2 CFA LS3-2.5: Defend the claim

**Target:** I can defend my claim against another explanation using the evidence.

**Question 1.** Scientists counted puppies with a new coat pattern that neither parent had.

| Kennel | Puppies checked | Puppies with a new coat pattern (in neither parent) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A (chemical in the water) | 1,000 | 14 |
| B (clean water) | 1,000 | 3 |

One student claims the chemical caused the extra new patterns. Another says copying errors caused all of them. Which evidence best defends the first claim?

- A. Both kennels had some puppies with a new coat pattern in them, so something about their DNA changed over time
- B. Kennel A had 14 new patterns per 1,000 puppies, while Kennel B, with the same copying errors, had only 3
- C. Copying errors happen in every dog, so they must be the reason for every new pattern in both kennels
- D. Kennel B's puppies have the same parents' alleles as Kennel A's puppies, so the two kennels match

**Question 2.** A student claims a puppy's new white patch came from a new combination of alleles. Which evidence would show this is **not** the best explanation?

- A. Both parents carry the white-patch allele in their DNA
- B. The puppy got one allele from each of its two parents
- C. Other puppies in the litter have different coat patterns
- D. The white-patch allele is found in neither parent

**Question 3.** A student claims a mutation in a dog's skin cell caused its puppy's new trait. Which statement best defends against this claim?

- A. Skin-cell mutations are not passed on, so the trait needs a change in a cell that makes eggs or sperm
- B. Skin cells have no DNA in them, so a skin cell cannot have a mutation that would ever affect any puppy
- C. Puppies get their traits only from their food, so no mutation in any cell can ever change a puppy's trait
- D. A mutation in one skin cell is always copied into every egg or sperm that the dog makes later in life

**Question 4.** True or false: To defend a claim, it is enough to restate the claim without citing any data.

**Question 5.** Select the **TWO** statements that make a strong defense of a claim against a counterclaim.

- A. It cites numbers from the data that support the claim
- B. It explains why the other explanation does not fit the data
- C. It says the claim is right because the student believes it is
- D. It ignores the counterclaim and repeats the claim more loudly

**Answer key**

| Question | Answer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | B | Copying errors should be about the same in both kennels. The extra patterns in Kennel A point to the chemical. |
| 2 | D | A new combination can only use alleles the parents already have. An allele neither parent has must be a mutation. |
| 3 | A | Only changes in cells that make eggs or sperm are inherited. |
| 4 | False | False. A defense cites evidence and shows why another explanation does not fit it. |
| 5 | A and B | A strong defense uses data and answers the other explanation. |
