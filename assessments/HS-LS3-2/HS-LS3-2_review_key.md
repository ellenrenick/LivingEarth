# HS-LS3-2 Review: Answer Key

Use with `HS-LS3-2_review.html`. Students do it on the review day, after the CFAs and before the CSA. Plan on 30 to 40 minutes. The Vocabulary and Write It sections are for everyone; students pick the other sections by their CFA scores (below 4 of 6).

## Vocabulary

1. genetic variation
2. allele
3. sex cell
4. meiosis
5. fertilization
6. mutation

## Section A: Three sources of variation

1. New combination of alleles.
2. Copying error (a mutation arising when DNA was copied).
3. Environmental mutation (UV light).
4. New combinations from meiosis and fertilization, copying errors, and mutations caused by the environment.

## Section B: Mixing alleles

1. One. Each can carry R or r.
2. RR, Rr, or rr.
3. Each sex cell gets a different mix of the parent's alleles, and fertilization joins two sex cells at random, so each brother gets a different new combination.

## Section C: Mutations and inheritance

1. No, not from skin cells. Only mutations in cells that make sex cells are passed to children.
2. Yes, because the change is in a sex cell.
3. Copying errors produce new variation in every generation.
4. False. Mutations can be harmful, helpful, or have no effect.

## Section D: Reading data as evidence

1. A new combination of alleles already in the parents.
2. A mutation, because neither parent has CCT.
3. A sequence in the child that is in neither parent, or a trait that never appeared in the family records.

## Section E: Make and defend a claim

1. X-rays caused mutations that were inherited: 55 of 1,000 offspring had the new color after X-rays compared with 3 of 1,000 in normal conditions.
2. Both groups ate the same food, so food cannot explain the difference; only the X-rays differed.
3. If pollution caused it, polluted ponds would have more spotted beetles. The same rate in clean ponds and parents who both carry the allele fit a new combination of existing alleles.

## Write It

**Checklist (what a strong answer includes):**

- Claims a mutation, most likely caused by the chemical
- Uses at least two data points (none in 15 generations; base change in neither parent; chemical exposure)
- Explains that the change was in a cell that makes sex cells so it could be inherited
- Explains why a new combination of existing alleles does not fit (neither parent has the sequence)
- Does not use phases of meiosis or chemistry details

**Model answer:** The orange spots are most likely a mutation caused by the chemical. Orange spots never appeared in the previous 15 generations, the tadpole has a base change that neither parent has, and the mother's eggs were exposed to a chemical that damages DNA. The chemical changed the DNA in a cell that became an egg, so the change was passed to the tadpole. It is not a new combination of alleles, because a new combination only mixes alleles the parents already have, and neither parent has this one.
