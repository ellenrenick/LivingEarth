### LS3-2.4

**Question 11.** Scientists exposed fruit flies to radiation and counted new mutations in the cells that make eggs.

| Group of fruit flies | Radiation dose | New mutations per 1,000 egg cells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | none | 4 |
| 2 | low | 15 |
| 3 | high | 31 |

Write a claim about what caused the new mutations. Support your claim with **two pieces of evidence** from the table, and explain your **reasoning** (how the evidence supports the claim and what the no-radiation group shows).

**Rubric (4 points)**

| Score | Level | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | Advanced | Claim: radiation caused (most of) the new mutations. Evidence: cites at least two numbers (for example 4 with no radiation, 15 with a low dose, 31 with a high dose) and notes the count rises with the dose. Reasoning: explains the no-radiation group shows the baseline from copying errors, and the extra mutations rise with the dose. |
| 3 | Proficient | Claim is correct. Cites at least one number from the table. Reasoning links the data to the claim but does not explain the baseline or the rise with dose. |
| 2 | Developing | Claim is correct but evidence is vague (no numbers) or reasoning is missing. |
| 1 | Beginning | Names a source of variation without using the table, or the claim does not match the data. |
| 0 | Blank or off-topic | |

**Grading notes**

- Accept "copying errors cause the baseline of 4" as a strong addition.
- A claim that only says "radiation" with no mention of the dose rising is a 2 or 3.
- Do not require the word "baseline." Look for the idea that the no-radiation group shows what copying errors alone do.

**Exemplar (score 4):** Claim: The radiation caused the extra mutations. Evidence: The flies with no radiation had 4 new mutations per 1,000 egg cells, but the high-dose flies had 31, and the low-dose flies had 15. Reasoning: The no-radiation group shows the mutations that copying errors make on their own. The count went up as the dose went up, so the radiation is what caused the extra mutations.

### LS3-2.5

**Question 13.** A farmer finds a calf with a new coat pattern that neither parent has. The farmer claims a chemical in the pasture's water caused a mutation. A veterinarian says it was a **copying error**, which happens in every herd.

| Herd | Calves checked | Calves with a new coat pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pasture with the chemical in its water | 2,000 | 6 |
| Barn with clean water | 2,000 | 5 |

Use the data and what you know about inheritance to **defend the better explanation**. Include (1) numbers from the table, (2) why the veterinarian's explanation fits the data better, and (3) in which kind of cell the change had to happen for the calf to have a pattern that neither parent has, and why.

**Rubric (4 points)**

| Score | Level | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | Advanced | Defends the veterinarian's explanation with all three parts: cites the numbers (6 vs 5 per 2,000, almost the same, so the chemical added few if any mutations); explains that copying errors happen at a similar low rate in both herds; and explains the change had to be in a cell that makes eggs or sperm, because only those changes are inherited. |
| 3 | Proficient | Defends the veterinarian's explanation, cites the numbers, and gives two of the three parts correctly. |
| 2 | Developing | Picks the veterinarian but the evidence or reasoning is vague, or only one part is correct. |
| 1 | Beginning | Picks a side without using the data or the inheritance idea, or picks the farmer's claim with no support. |
| 0 | Blank or off-topic | |

**Grading notes**

- The pattern is new in the calf and neither parent has it, so the change must have been in a cell that makes eggs or sperm in one of the parents. A change in any other body cell could not be passed on.
- Accept "6 and 5 are almost the same" for the data point. Do not require the word "baseline."
- Give credit for noting the data cannot rule out that a few of the 6 came from the chemical, because the difference is small.

**Exemplar (score 4):** The veterinarian's explanation fits better. The pasture with the chemical had 6 calves with a new pattern out of 2,000, and the barn with clean water had 5 out of 2,000. That is almost the same, so the chemical did not add many mutations. Copying errors happen in every herd at a similar low rate, which fits both numbers. For a calf to have a pattern that neither parent has, the change had to happen in a cell that makes eggs or sperm, because only those changes are inherited.
