# HS-LS4-4 Review: Answer Key

Use with `HS-LS4-4_review.html`. Students do it on the review day, after the CFAs and before the CSA. Plan on 30 to 40 minutes. The Vocabulary and Write It sections are for everyone; students pick the other sections by their CFA scores (below 4 of 6).

## Vocabulary

1. natural selection
2. adaptation
3. population
4. biotic factor
5. abiotic factors
6. allele frequency

## Section A: Key terms in action

1. Biotic: predator, food plant, competing species. Abiotic: ocean acidity, temperature.
2. An inherited trait that helps an organism survive and reproduce in its environment.
3. Individuals with helpful inherited traits survive and reproduce more, so those traits become more common.

## Section B: How selection causes a population to evolve

1. Variation in a trait, the trait is inherited, and differences in survival or reproduction.
2. Giraffes varied in neck length. Those with longer, inherited necks reached more food, survived, and had more offspring, so long necks became common.
3. Bacteria that already had the trait survived and reproduced, so it became common.

## Section C: Using data to show a shift

1. Thick fur rose from 10% in generation 0 to 80% in generation 15.
2. (80 - 10) / 3 = about 23 percentage points per 5 generations.
3. Thick-furred mice survived the cold and reproduced more, so the inherited trait became common.

## Section D: Better suited to the environment

1. Owls caught mice that stood out, so mice matching their background survived and reproduced more in each area.
2. Its common fur color matches the ground, so it is better hidden.
3. Dark mice would stand out and be eaten more, so light fur would become more common.

## Write It

**Checklist (what a strong answer includes):**

- States that early flowering became more common through natural selection
- Uses at least two numbers (80% versus 20%; 10% to 70%)
- Names drought (rainfall) as the abiotic factor
- Explains variation, inheritance, and differences in seed-setting
- Predicts that the advantage would weaken if rainfall returned

**Model answer:** Natural selection made early flowering more common. 80% of early-flowering plants set seed before the dry season compared with 20% of late-flowering plants, and the early-flowering share of the population rose from 10% to 70% in 6 years. Drought, an abiotic factor, killed plants that had not set seed yet. Flowering time varies and is inherited, so early plants left more offspring that inherited early flowering, which makes it an adaptation. If rainfall returned to normal, late flowering would no longer be a disadvantage, so the share of early-flowering plants would stop rising.
