## Section A: Population, biodiversity, and the factors that change them

1. 30 oaks is a population; 40 insect species relates to biodiversity.
2. Examples: a new predator, pollution, habitat loss, an invasive species.

## Section B: Reading tables and graphs

1. 120.
2. The population increased from 2020 to 2022 and then leveled off in 2023.

## Section C: Mean, rate of change, and trend

1. (90 + 120 + 150 + 150) / 4 = 127.5.
2. (150 - 90) / 2 = 30 frogs per year.
3. Lake A at 20 per year; Lake B grew 15 per year.

## Section D: Comparing ecosystems of different sizes

1. Pond: 40 / 0.5 = 80. Lake: 90 / 5 = 18.
2. The pond (80 versus 18 species per km<sup>2</sup>), even though the lake has more species in total.
3. So ecosystems of different sizes can be compared fairly.

## Section E: Math as evidence, and revising

1. Groups 1 and 4 (road versus no road, with no pesticide) and groups 2 and 3.
2. Road alone: -3%, very small. Pesticide runoff: about -60% with or without a road.
3. Frog populations dropped about 60% in ponds with pesticide runoff, with or without a road, so pesticide runoff is the main factor.

## Write It

**Checklist (what a strong answer includes):**

- States that cats reduce bird biodiversity
- Shows the four calculations (W 8, X 4, Y 3, Z 1.8 species per km<sup>2</sup>)
- Compares islands of the same size (W with Y, X with Z)
- Explains that dividing by area makes different-sized islands comparable
- Gives a reason, such as cats eating birds and eggs

**Model answer:** Invasive cats reduce bird biodiversity. W has 32 / 4 = 8 species per km<sup>2</sup>, X has 80 / 20 = 4, Y has 12 / 4 = 3, and Z has 36 / 20 = 1.8. Comparing same-sized islands, W (no cats) has 8 and Y (cats) has 3, and X (no cats) has 4 and Z (cats) has 1.8, so cats lower biodiversity by more than half. I divided by area because a larger island is expected to have more species just from its size. Cats eat birds and their eggs, so some species cannot reproduce enough to stay on the island.
