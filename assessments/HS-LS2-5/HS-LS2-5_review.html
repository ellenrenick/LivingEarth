Key ideas to know

  1. Four spheres store carbon: the atmosphere (CO2 in the air), biosphere (living things), hydrosphere (dissolved in water), and geosphere (rocks, soil, fossil fuels).
  2. Photosynthesis uses light energy to move carbon from CO2 in the atmosphere (or water) into sugar in living things.
  3. Cellular respiration releases the energy stored in sugar and returns carbon to the atmosphere as CO2. Plants and animals both do it, all the time.
  4. Other processes move carbon too: burning, decay, burial in rock, and CO2 dissolving in or leaving the ocean.
  5. Carbon is conserved. It changes places and forms but is never created or destroyed. Light is energy, not carbon.
  6. If one part of the cycle changes, carbon builds up or drops elsewhere. Use the arrows in a model to predict where.

Section A: Spheres and other carbon-moving processes

Name where carbon is stored and the processes, besides photosynthesis and respiration, that move it.

  1. In which sphere is the carbon in each: (a) coal, (b) CO2 gas in the air, (c) a maple tree, (d) CO2 dissolved in the ocean?
  2. Complete the table: for each process, give the sphere carbon moves from and to. Burning gasoline; burial of dead sea creatures under mud; decay of fallen leaves; CO2 dissolving into the ocean.
  3. True or false: When wood burns, the carbon in it is destroyed. Explain.

Section B: Photosynthesis and cellular respiration

A plant in a sealed jar with a CO2 sensor: bright light, CO2 fell from 800 to 520 ppm in 2 hours. In the dark, CO2 rose from 800 to 950 ppm.

  1. Explain the drop in CO2 in the light.
  2. Explain the rise in the dark.
  3. Fill in: Photosynthesis moves carbon from the ____________ to the ____________. Cellular respiration moves it from the ____________ back to the ____________.
  4. A student says only animals do cellular respiration. What evidence shows she is wrong?

Section C: Trace a carbon atom

A carbon atom is in a CO2 molecule in the air. It enters a grass leaf, is eaten by a cow, and the cow dies and is buried and slowly becomes part of the rock.

  1. List the sphere at each step, in order.
  2. Name the process that moves the atom at each step.
  3. Write your own path for one carbon atom through at least three different spheres, naming each process.

Section D: Using and building a model

A model of the carbon cycle needs all four spheres, arrows that show which way carbon moves, and labels for each process.

  1. Build a model in words and arrows. Include all four spheres, photosynthesis, cellular respiration, and at least one more process.
  2. Predict: Ocean algae die off in a warming sea. Use your model to say what happens to carbon in the atmosphere.
  3. Predict: Fewer dead plants and animals are buried each year. What changes in the geosphere and the atmosphere?
  4. A model shows only an arrow from the air to plants and one from plants to the air. List two things that would make it more complete.
