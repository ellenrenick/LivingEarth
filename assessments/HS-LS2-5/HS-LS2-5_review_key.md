# HS-LS2-5 Review: Answer Key

Use with `HS-LS2-5_review.html`. Students do it on the review day, after the CFAs and before the CSA. Plan on 30 to 40 minutes. The Vocabulary and Write It sections are for everyone; students pick the other sections by their CFA scores (below 4 of 6).

## Vocabulary

1. biosphere
2. atmosphere
3. hydrosphere
4. geosphere
5. photosynthesis
6. cellular respiration

## Section A: Spheres and other carbon-moving processes

1. (a) geosphere, (b) atmosphere, (c) biosphere, (d) hydrosphere.
2. Burning gasoline: geosphere to atmosphere. Burial: biosphere to geosphere. Decay: biosphere to atmosphere and soil (geosphere). Dissolving: atmosphere to hydrosphere.
3. False. Carbon is conserved. It moves from the wood (biosphere) into the air as CO<sub>2</sub>.

## Section B: Photosynthesis and cellular respiration

1. Photosynthesis took in more CO<sub>2</sub> than the plant's respiration gave off, moving carbon from the air into sugar.
2. There is no photosynthesis in the dark, but the plant's cells still do cellular respiration, which gives off CO<sub>2</sub>.
3. Atmosphere, biosphere (living things); biosphere, atmosphere.
4. A plant in the dark gives off CO<sub>2</sub>, which shows plant cells do cellular respiration too.

## Section C: Trace a carbon atom

1. Atmosphere, biosphere (grass), biosphere (cow), geosphere (buried and in rock).
2. Photosynthesis (air to grass), eating (grass to cow), burial (cow to rock).
3. Example: atmosphere to biosphere by photosynthesis; biosphere to hydrosphere as a leaf is washed into a lake; hydrosphere to geosphere by burial at the bottom of the lake. Any correct path through three spheres is acceptable.

## Section D: Using and building a model

1. Atmosphere to biosphere (photosynthesis); biosphere to atmosphere (respiration); atmosphere to and from hydrosphere (dissolving); biosphere to geosphere (burial); geosphere to atmosphere (burning fossil fuels).
2. Less photosynthesis in the ocean removes less CO<sub>2</sub>, so more carbon stays in the atmosphere.
3. Less carbon moves into the geosphere, so more stays cycling through living things and the air, and atmospheric CO<sub>2</sub> may rise.
4. Add the ocean and rocks (hydrosphere, geosphere) and processes like dissolving, burial, and burning.

## Write It

**Checklist (what a strong answer includes):**

- States that carbon moved from the soil (geosphere) into the atmosphere
- Uses at least two numbers from the table
- Names the spheres involved and the process (decay and respiration by soil organisms)
- Says carbon is moved, not destroyed
- Predicts that replanting grass would take carbon back into plants and soil through photosynthesis

**Model answer:** The plowing moved carbon from the soil into the air. The carbon stored in the soil fell from 80 to 51 tons per hectare in 10 years, while the soil released 4.1 tons per hectare to the air in year 5. Plowing exposes the soil, so decomposers break down dead plant matter faster and give off CO<sub>2</sub> by cellular respiration, moving carbon from the geosphere to the atmosphere. The carbon was not destroyed; it changed spheres. If grass were replanted, photosynthesis would take CO<sub>2</sub> back out of the air into plants and, over time, into the soil.
