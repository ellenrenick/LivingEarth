# HS-LS4-1 Review: Answer Key

Use with `HS-LS4-1_review.html`. Students do it on the review day, after the CFAs and before the CSA. Plan on 30 to 40 minutes. The Vocabulary and Write It sections are for everyone; students pick the other sections by their CFA scores (below 4 of 6).

## Vocabulary

1. common ancestry
2. evolution
3. homologous structures
4. vestigial structure
5. fossil record
6. embryonic development

## Section A: Kinds of evidence for common ancestry

1. DNA similarity, shared body structures, and early embryo development.
2. The chimpanzee is most closely related, then the mouse, then the chicken, because more similar DNA means a more recent common ancestor.
3. The structure was inherited from a common ancestor and modified for different uses.
4. They inherited these early features from a common ancestor.

## Section B: Explaining several lines together

1. DNA: closer to land mammals. Fossil: the ancestor walked on land. Embryo buds: leftovers from legged ancestors.
2. They are independent lines of evidence that agree, so the conclusion is more reliable.
3. Looks alone can be misleading. Check DNA, embryos, and skeleton.
4. Example: Related species have similar DNA and similar bones, because they inherited them from a common ancestor. When both agree, we can be more sure.

## Section C: Evidence for evolution

1. Traits changed gradually over time, with intermediate forms.
2. A vestigial structure, a leftover from ancestors that had legs.
3. Fossil record and DNA similarity (or homologous structures, vestigial structures, embryos).

## Section D: Putting it together

1. Humans and chimpanzees descended from a common ancestor millions of years ago. Both lines kept evolving.
2. Closer relatives in the sequence would have more similar DNA.
3. Fossils show living things changing over time, and DNA shows closer relatives have more similar genes. Because different evidence agrees, scientists are confident.

## Write It

**Checklist (what a strong answer includes):**

- States the claim in plain language that birds share a common ancestor with dinosaurs
- Uses at least three different lines of evidence from the table
- Explains what each line shows about ancestry
- Explains why several independent lines are stronger than one
- Does not say individuals changed on purpose or in their lifetimes

**Model answer:** Birds are living relatives of dinosaurs and share a common ancestor with them. Fossils show dinosaurs with feathers and wings and early birds with teeth and bony tails, which are intermediate forms. A bird's wing has the same bone pattern as the forelimb of a meat-eating dinosaur, and fossil nests show some dinosaurs sat on eggs the way birds do. Bird DNA is also more similar to crocodile DNA than to lizard DNA. Each kind of evidence points to the same answer, so together they are much stronger than any one alone.
