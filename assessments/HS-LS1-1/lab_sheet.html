Gummy DNA Lab: Build, Copy, and Read a Gene

Name: ______________   Partner: ______________

Question: How does a cell's copy of a gene become a chain of amino acids, and what happens if one letter of DNA changes?

You will build a short model of the start of a cat's gene for a pigment-making protein. (The letters are an illustration, not the real gene.)

Materials (per pair)

  • 2 licorice strands (the backbone; the sugars and phosphates)
  • Candy “bases”: 6 red (A), 6 yellow (T), 6 green (C), 6 orange (G) gummies, plus 2 extra of each color
  • Toothpicks (to attach bases to the backbone and to hold pairs together)
  • Paper plate or tray, this sheet, the code table

Safety: Keep materials clean. Do not eat the lab materials until your teacher says you may. Toothpicks are sharp. Tell your teacher about any food allergy. If candy is not used, use colored beads or paper circles.

Part A: Build the template strand (6 min)

This is the strand of the gene that the cell uses as a template. Attach the bases to one licorice strand in order from left to right:

T A C   C C T   G A C   A A A

Write the color order you built: ______________________________________________

Part B: Build the partner strand and make the ladder (8 min)

Build the partner strand on the second licorice strand by pairing each base (A with T, C with G). Write the partner sequence you built:

_ _ _   _ _ _   _ _ _   _ _ _

  1. Join the two strands with toothpicks between pairs. Gently twist the ladder. Have your teacher check it before you continue. ☐ Checked
  2. Which strand carries the “coding” letters (the same as the cell's message copy)? How do you know?

Part C: Copy the message, then read it (10 min)

The cell does not use the DNA directly to make a protein. It copies the gene's message from the template strand, and then the copy is read to link amino acids. The DNA stays safe.

  1. Pull the two strands apart. Keep the template strand. Build a new strand by pairing with the template. This is the copy. Write its sequence: ___ ___ ___ ___
  2. Read the copy three letters at a time with the code table. Write the amino acid chain: ______ – ______ – ______ – ______
  3. Line up one bead or paper tag per amino acid on the plate in order. This chain would fold to make a protein.
  4. Explain the path in your own words, starting with the DNA: ______________________________________________

Part D: Change one letter (7 min)

  1. On your template strand, replace the 4th base (the first C of the second triplet) with a T (swap a green gummy for a yellow one). The template now reads TAC TCT GAC AAA.
  2. Build the new copy by pairing with the changed template. Sequence: ___ ___ ___ ___
  3. Read it with the code table: ______ – ______ – ______ – ______
  4. Compare with Part C. How many amino acids changed? Which one?
  5. Predict: Could a protein built from this changed chain work differently? Explain.

Connect to the Siamese cat

  1. In a Siamese cat, a one-letter change in the gene for a pigment-making protein swaps one amino acid. In your own words, what did your one-letter change do to the chain in the model?
  2. Name two ways your gummy model is different from real DNA.

Clean up

Put toothpicks and candy in the trash or the labeled container. Wipe your table. Wash your hands.
