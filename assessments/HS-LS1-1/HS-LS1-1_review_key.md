# HS-LS1-1 Review: Answer Key

Use with `HS-LS1-1_review.html`. Students do it on the review day, after the CFAs and before the CSA. Plan on 30 to 40 minutes. The Vocabulary and Write It sections are for everyone; students pick the other sections by their CFA scores (below 4 of 6).

## Vocabulary

1. DNA
2. nucleotide
3. gene
4. base sequence
5. amino acids
6. protein

## Section A: DNA and genes

1. A double helix (twisted ladder). A nucleotide has a sugar, a phosphate, and a base.
2. A, T, C, and G.
3. A protein.
4. False. DNA holds the instructions, and proteins do the jobs for the cell.

## Section B: From gene to amino acid chain

1. DNA; a gene; the copy of the gene's message; the protein.
2. (c), then (b), then (a).
3. Met, Leu, Phe, Pro.
4. Met, Gly, Asp.

## Section C: What a base change can do

1. Original: Met, Glu, Leu. Changed: Met, Val, Leu.
2. One amino acid changed (Glu to Val). A chain with a different amino acid can make a protein that works differently.
3. Not every change matters. A and B did not stop the protein. What matters is whether the amino acid chain changes and how.

## Section D: Same DNA, different jobs

1. Yes. Both have genes 1 to 4.
2. Each cell type uses a different set of genes.
3. Cell P uses genes 1 and 2 to build proteins 1 and 2 that carry signals; Cell Q uses genes 3 and 4 to build proteins 3 and 4 that squeeze. The proteins carry out each cell's job.
4. Cell Q cannot do its job well, because it depends on Protein 3.

## Section E: A case study

1. (b), (d), (c), (a).
2. A single base change altered one amino acid, so the protein works poorly; because the protein does not do its job, the person has symptoms.

## Write It

**Checklist (what a strong answer includes):**

- States that one base change led to a different amino acid and a protein that does not work
- Uses at least two pieces of data (GAG versus GTG; Glu versus Val; works versus does not)
- Describes copy-then-read from the gene to an amino acid chain
- Links the base sequence to the amino acid order
- Explains that a protein that does not work means the cell cannot do its job

**Model answer:** One base in the gene changed, which changed one amino acid, so the protein no longer works. Person 1's gene reads ATG GAG GAC CTG and Person 2's reads ATG GTG GAC CTG, so GAG (Glu) is GTG (Val) in Person 2, and the protein does not work. A cell copies the message of a gene and reads the copy to link amino acids in order, so the base sequence sets the amino acid order. A chain with a different amino acid can make a protein that works differently. Since this protein cannot help the cell take in iron, the cell cannot do its job.
