## Section A: Roles in an ecosystem

1. Grass: producer. Rabbit: consumer. Hawk: consumer. Mushroom: decomposer.
2. It would stay locked in the dead matter and not return to the soil and air for producers to reuse.

## Section B: Matter cycles, energy flows

1. Recycled (cycles); flows one way and leaves as heat.
2. The Sun.
3. Decomposers return matter, not energy. Energy is lost as heat, so producers need new energy from the Sun.

## Section C: Energy pyramids and the 10% rule

1. 10% each time (2,000/20,000, 200/2,000, 20/200).
2. It is used for life processes such as moving and growing, or lost as heat.
3. 800 kJ, then 80 kJ.
4. 45 / 500 = 0.09 = 9%.
5. So little energy is left at the top (only about 10% per step) that it cannot support more levels.

## Section D: Matter is conserved

1. 1,000 at the start and 1,000 at the end.
2. Matter (carbon) is conserved; it moved among the air, plants, animals, and soil but was not created or destroyed.
3. Total 1,000. Plants = 1,000 - 470 - 215 = 315.

## Section E: Supporting a claim with math

1. 100,000 &times; 0.1 &times; 0.1 &times; 0.1 = 100 kJ at the hawk level; 100 / 25 = 4 hawks.
2. Claim: Energy decreases at each level. If producers store 30,000 kJ, the next level stores about 3,000 kJ and the next about 300 kJ.

## Write It

**Checklist (what a strong answer includes):**

- Calculates the totals (100 on day 0 and 100 on day 20) and says matter is conserved
- Explains that carbon moved among the parts but was not created or destroyed
- Explains the roles of producers, consumers, and decomposers
- Says energy must keep entering as light
- Predicts that in the dark the producers stop growing and the ecosystem fails, though the carbon is still there

**Model answer:** The total carbon is 70 + 20 + 4 + 6 = 100 units on day 0 and 58 + 29 + 5 + 8 = 100 units on day 20, so matter is conserved: carbon moved from the air and water into the algae, snails, and mud, but none was lost or made. The algae are producers that take in CO<sub>2</sub> and build their bodies using light, the snails are consumers that eat the algae, and the bacteria are decomposers that break down dead matter and return carbon to the water. In the dark, the algae could not capture energy, so they would stop growing and the other organisms would run out of food. The carbon would still be in the jar, but energy has to keep flowing in to run the cycle.
