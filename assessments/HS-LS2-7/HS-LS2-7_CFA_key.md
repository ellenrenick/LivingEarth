## LS2-7.1 (DOK 2, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can explain how human activities like pollution and deforestation affect ecosystems and biodiversity.

**Q1.** How does deforestation usually affect biodiversity?

A. It increases habitat for all species.  
B. It has no effect.  
C. It makes new species appear immediately.  
D. It removes habitat, so fewer species can live there.  ✅  

*Answer D.* Clearing forest removes habitat.

**Q2.** Fertilizer washing into a lake causes algae to grow too much. What happens next?

A. The algae die and decompose, using up oxygen so fish may die.  ✅  
B. More oxygen is made and fish multiply.  
C. The lake becomes clearer.  
D. Nothing changes.  

*Answer A.* Decomposing algae use up oxygen.

**Q3.** Explain in 1 or 2 sentences how one human activity (such as pollution, a dam, or an invasive species) affects an ecosystem.

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Names an activity and a specific effect on the ecosystem or biodiversity. |
| 1 | Names an activity or an effect, but they are not connected. |
| 0 | Blank or incorrect. |

*Model answer:* A dam blocks salmon from swimming upstream to lay eggs, so the number of salmon drops.

## LS2-7.2 (DOK 3, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can design a solution, based on evidence, that reduces a human impact on the environment.

**Q1.** Which is the best first step in designing a solution to reduce a human impact?

A. Ignore the data.  
B. Pick the cheapest idea.  
C. Pick the idea you like best.  
D. Use data to identify the biggest cause of the problem.  ✅  

*Answer D.* Designs should be based on evidence about the cause.

**Q2.** Farm runoff causes 60% of a lake's pollution and lawns cause 10%. Which design is most likely to help most?

A. Reduce runoff from farms.  ✅  
B. Reduce runoff from lawns only.  
C. Add more fertilizer.  
D. Do nothing.  

*Answer A.* Targeting the biggest cause gives the biggest benefit.

**Q3.** A river has too much trash from storm drains. Describe a solution and the evidence you would use to choose it (2 sentences).

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Describes a solution that addresses the cause and cites the kind of evidence (data) that supports it. |
| 1 | Describes a solution without evidence. |
| 0 | Blank or incorrect. |

*Model answer:* I would put trash screens in storm drains, because counts show most trash enters the river through them.

## LS2-7.3 (DOK 3, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can judge whether a solution will work, and what it gains and costs.

**Q1.** A solution cuts pollution by 60% and costs $50,000. The goal is a 50% cut for under $100,000. What is the best judgment?

A. It meets the cost but not the pollution goal.  
B. It meets the pollution goal but not the cost.  
C. It does not meet either goal.  
D. It meets the goals.  ✅  

*Answer D.* 60% is at least 50%, and $50,000 is under $100,000.

**Q2.** A solution gives up some jobs but protects a forest. This is an example of a

A. trade-off  ✅  
B. constraint  
C. criterion  
D. hypothesis  

*Answer A.* A trade-off gives up one thing to gain another.

**Q3.** Solution A cuts pollution by 70% at $200,000. Solution B cuts it by 40% at $20,000. The goal is at least 50%. Which meets the goal, and what is its cost? (1 or 2 sentences)

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Chooses A and names the cost ($200,000) or the trade-off. |
| 1 | Chooses A with no reasoning, or reasons without a choice. |
| 0 | Blank or incorrect. |

*Model answer:* Solution A meets the 50% goal, but it costs $200,000, which is the trade-off.

## LS2-7.4 (DOK 4, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can use feedback and new evidence to improve my solution.

**Q1.** A test shows a solution worked in two of three towns. What should the designers do?

A. Stop all work.  
B. Repeat exactly the same design.  
C. Ignore the third town.  
D. Find out why it failed in the third town and change the design.  ✅  

*Answer D.* Evidence from a test should guide improvements.

**Q2.** Reviewers say your solution is too expensive. Which response is best?

A. Look for a cheaper way to meet the same goal and test it.  ✅  
B. Make the solution more expensive.  
C. Say the reviewers are wrong.  
D. Ignore the feedback.  

*Answer A.* Feedback should be used to improve the design.

**Q3.** A new data set shows your solution works poorly in the dry season. Describe one change you would make and how you would check it (1 or 2 sentences).

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Describes a specific change tied to the new data and a way to check it. |
| 1 | Gives a change without a way to check, or a vague change. |
| 0 | Blank or incorrect. |

*Model answer:* I would add a way to store water for the dry season and then measure the results next dry season.

## LS2-7.5 (DOK 4, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can present and defend my solution with evidence.

**Q1.** Which is the strongest way to defend a solution?

A. Say it is the best.  
B. Say everyone likes it.  
C. Say it is natural.  
D. Use data about how well it works and its cost, and address a limit.  ✅  

*Answer D.* Evidence and honesty about limits make a strong defense.

**Q2.** A classmate says your solution is too costly. Which reply uses evidence?

A. It costs $40,000 and removes 70% of the pollution, which is cheaper per percent than the other options.  ✅  
B. I do not have an answer.  
C. It is a good idea.  
D. Costs do not matter.  

*Answer A.* Specific numbers support the defense.

**Q3.** In 2 sentences, defend a solution to reduce plastic in a stream. Use at least one piece of evidence.

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | States the solution and supports it with evidence. |
| 1 | States the solution without evidence. |
| 0 | Blank or incorrect. |

*Model answer:* Putting trash screens on storm drains will keep plastic out of the stream. In a test, screens caught 80% of the trash before it reached the stream.
