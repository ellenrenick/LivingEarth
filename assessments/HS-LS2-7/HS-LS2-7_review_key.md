# HS-LS2-7 Review: Answer Key

Use with `HS-LS2-7_review.html`. Students do it on the review day, after the CFAs and before the CSA. Plan on 30 to 40 minutes. The Vocabulary and Write It sections are for everyone; students pick the other sections by their CFA scores (below 4 of 6).

## Vocabulary

1. deforestation
2. pollution
3. invasive species
4. criteria
5. constraints
6. trade-off

## Section A: How human activities affect ecosystems

1. As forest was cleared from 100% to 30%, bird species fell from 70 to 20.
2. Fertilizer runoff adds nutrients, algae grow too much, the algae die and decompose and use up oxygen, and fish die.
3. It falls sharply because salmon cannot reach their spawning areas.

## Section B: Designing a solution with evidence

1. Storm drains, because they are the largest source (55%).
2. Install trash screens in storm drains.
3. Criterion: reduce trash by at least 50%. Constraint: cost no more than $100,000 per year.

## Section C: Judging gains and costs

1. No. It meets the cost but reduces only 45%, below 50%.
2. A + B: 65% for $65,000. A + C: 57% for $60,000. A + B + C: 77% for $75,000.
3. It costs $120,000, over the budget, and reduces only 25%.
4. Example: A + B costs more than A alone but cuts trash much more.

## Section D: Refining and defending

1. Add more screens or add a solution for another source, then measure trash again next year and compare with the 50% goal.
2. Example: The plan cuts trash by 65% for $65,000, under the $100,000 limit, which is cheaper per percent than street sweeping.
3. Example: Screens need cleaning, so schedule monthly maintenance.

## Write It

**Checklist (what a strong answer includes):**

- Chooses a combination that meets both criteria and shows the totals
- Compares costs and reductions (for example, cost per percent)
- Explains why at least one option was left out (D is over budget)
- Names a specific change tied to the feedback
- Says how to test the change and compare with the 40% goal

**Model answer:** I choose A, B, and C: 22% + 18% + 14% = 54% reduction, and $50,000 + $80,000 + $8,000 = $138,000 per year, which meets both criteria. A targets farms, the largest source (45%); C is the cheapest per percent (about $570 per percent) and B costs more per percent (about $4,400) but adds protection, which is a trade-off. I left out D because at $220,000 it is over the budget by itself. If nitrogen fell only 30%, I would add strips along more farm streams, since farms are the biggest source, and test it by measuring nitrogen in the bay next summer and comparing it with the 40% goal.
