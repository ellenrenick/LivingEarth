# HS-ESS3-1 Review: Answer Key

Use with `HS-ESS3-1_review.html`. Students do it on the review day, after the CFAs and before the CSA. Plan on 30 to 40 minutes. The Vocabulary and Write It sections are for everyone; students pick the other sections by their CFA scores (below 4 of 6).

## Vocabulary

1. natural resource
2. natural hazard
3. fertile soil
4. climate
5. migration
6. sea level rise

## Section A: Resources, hazards, and climate factors

1. Resources: fertile soil, a river. Hazards: earthquake, hurricane. Climate factors: less rainfall, rising sea level.
2. It is spread unevenly: Region A has 30 times as much as Region C.
3. Region C, because it has the least fresh water per person.

## Section B: How hazards shape where and how people live

1. Fertile soil and nearby water make farming productive, and the benefits outweigh the flood risk for them.
2. Earthquake-resistant buildings, levees, early-warning systems, or raised homes.
3. Floods damage property (hazard) but leave nutrient-rich mud that renews the soil (resource).

## Section C: Climate change, crops, and migration

1. Both fell: rainfall from 800 to 500 mm, yield from 6.0 to 2.5 tons per hectare.
2. Switch to crops that need less water, irrigate, or move away.
3. Build sea walls, raise buildings, move to higher ground, or relocate inland.

## Section D: Reading data and maps

1. The cities nearest water are the largest; the farthest is the smallest.
2. More rainfall goes with larger populations, because water supports farming and cities.
3. City P is 3 km from water and has 8.0 million people, while City R is 150 km away and has 0.2 million.

## Section E: Putting it together with evidence

1. Volcanic ash makes the soil very fertile, which gives good harvests, and the benefit outweighs the hazard for them.
2. A change in climate and a resource can change what people do.
3. People live on flood plains, volcano slopes, and earthquake zones because of fertile soil, ports, and water, so they accept hazards for resources.

## Write It

**Checklist (what a strong answer includes):**

- States that the benefits of the slopes outweigh the hazard for many people
- Uses at least two numbers (9 versus 4 tons; 800 versus 120 people; 2 eruptions)
- Names the resource (fertile volcanic soil), the hazard (eruptions), and the climate change (falling rainfall)
- Explains how each could influence people's future choices
- Does not say the area is safe

**Model answer:** Many more people live on the slopes because the fertile volcanic soil makes farming much more productive, even though eruptions are a hazard. The slopes yield 9 tons per hectare compared with 4 on the plain, and they hold 800 people per km<sup>2</sup> compared with 120. The fertile soil is the resource, the eruptions that damaged farms twice a century are the hazard, and rainfall falling 6 mm per year is a climate change. Because the benefits are large, people accept the hazard and prepare for it, but if rainfall keeps falling, farmers may need irrigation or drought-tolerant crops, and some people may move.
