## ESS3-1.1 (DOK 1, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can name the resources, hazards, and climate factors that shape where people live.

**Q1.** Which is a natural **resource**?

A. Fresh water  ✅  
B. A hurricane  
C. An earthquake  
D. A flood warning  

*Answer A.* Fresh water is a resource people use.

**Q2.** Which is a natural **hazard**?

A. Iron ore  
B. A volcanic eruption  ✅  
C. A river  
D. Fertile soil  

*Answer B.* Volcanic eruptions can harm people.

**Q3.** Which change in **climate** can affect where people live?

A. A road  
B. A market  
C. Rising sea level  ✅  
D. A new school  

*Answer C.* Rising seas can flood coasts and push people to move.

## ESS3-1.2 (DOK 2, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can describe how resources like water and fertile soil are spread unevenly.

**Q1.** Resources such as fresh water are

A. spread unevenly across Earth  ✅  
B. spread evenly everywhere  
C. found only in deserts  
D. not needed by people  

*Answer A.* Resources are concentrated in some places.

**Q2.** Why do large river deltas often have many farms?

A. They have no water.  
B. Rivers deposit fertile soil and bring fresh water.  ✅  
C. They are always dry.  
D. The soil is poor.  

*Answer B.* Deltas have fertile soil and water.

**Q3.** Region A has 8,000 and Region B has 400 cubic meters of fresh water per person each year. Describe in 1 sentence how this affects where people can live and farm.

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Says the water is uneven and Region B has less water for farming or cities, citing the numbers. |
| 1 | Says the water is uneven without the effect on people. |
| 0 | Blank or incorrect. |

*Model answer:* Region B has only 400 cubic meters per person compared with 8,000 in Region A, so it can support less farming and fewer people.

## ESS3-1.3 (DOK 2, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can describe how hazards like earthquakes and floods affect where and how people live.

**Q1.** People who live in an earthquake zone often

A. build earthquake-resistant buildings  ✅  
B. never use the land  
C. avoid all buildings  
D. ignore the hazard completely  

*Answer A.* People adapt by building safer structures.

**Q2.** Why might people choose to live on a flood plain?

A. There are no floods.  
B. The fertile soil and nearby water outweigh the flood risk for them.  ✅  
C. It is the highest land.  
D. The soil is poor.  

*Answer B.* People weigh benefits against risks.

**Q3.** Describe one way a hazard (such as a flood or earthquake) changes where or how people live.

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Describes a specific change, such as moving, building differently, or avoiding the hazard zone, and links it to the hazard. |
| 1 | Names an effect without linking it to the hazard. |
| 0 | Blank or incorrect. |

*Model answer:* In earthquake zones, people build with stronger materials, and after floods some people move to higher ground.

## ESS3-1.4 (DOK 2, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can explain how climate change can move people and change what they grow.

**Q1.** If a region becomes much drier, farmers may

A. switch to crops that need less water or move away  ✅  
B. not change anything  
C. stop eating  
D. grow more water-loving crops  

*Answer A.* Climate changes push farmers to adapt or leave.

**Q2.** Sea level rise can push people to leave coastal areas because

A. oceans shrink  
B. more land floods more often  ✅  
C. the land rises  
D. storms stop  

*Answer B.* Higher seas flood low-lying land.

**Q3.** Explain in 1 or 2 sentences how a hotter, drier climate could change what a region grows and where people live.

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Links the climate change to crops and to people moving or staying. |
| 1 | Gives one of the two. |
| 0 | Blank or incorrect. |

*Model answer:* A hotter, drier climate means farmers must grow crops that need less water, and some people may move to places with more water.

## ESS3-1.5 (DOK 2, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can read maps and data that connect resources, hazards, or climate to human activity.

**Q1.** | City | Distance to river (km) | Population (millions) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 1 | 5 |
| B | 80 | 0.3 |

What does the table suggest?

A. Cities near a river tend to be larger.  ✅  
B. Both have equal populations.  
C. Cities far from rivers are larger.  
D. Distance does not matter.  

*Answer A.* The city by the river has more people.

**Q2.** A map shows most large cities along coasts and rivers. This suggests

A. rivers cause cities to disappear  
B. people settle where water and transport are available  ✅  
C. cities are placed at random  
D. people avoid water  

*Answer B.* Water and trade routes support settlement.

**Q3.** Use the table above to write one sentence connecting a resource to human activity. Include a number.

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Connects the river resource to the larger population with a number. |
| 1 | Connects without a number. |
| 0 | Blank or incorrect. |

*Model answer:* City A is 1 km from a river and has 5 million people, while City B is 80 km away and has only 0.3 million.

## ESS3-1.6 (DOK 3, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can use evidence to explain how resources, hazards, and climate have shaped human activity.

**Q1.** Which statement best uses evidence to explain why a city grew near a volcano?

A. Volcanic ash made the soil fertile, so farming supported many people despite the eruption hazard.  ✅  
B. Nobody lives near volcanoes.  
C. People enjoy danger.  
D. Volcanoes never erupt.  

*Answer A.* The benefit of fertile soil outweighs the hazard for many people.

**Q2.** A drought cut a river's flow in half and the town lost half its farms. What does this show?

A. The town did not change.  
B. A change in climate and resources can change human activity.  ✅  
C. Farms never depend on water.  
D. Rivers do not matter.  

*Answer B.* Less water led to fewer farms.

**Q3.** Choose a place (real or from class). In 2 or 3 sentences, use one resource, one hazard, and one climate factor to explain how people live there.

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Names all three accurately and links each to what people do. |
| 1 | Names two, or names three without linking. |
| 0 | Blank or incorrect. |

*Model answer:* In a river delta, fertile soil and water (resources) let people farm. Floods (a hazard) happen yearly, so people build raised homes, and sea level rise (a climate change) may force some to move.
