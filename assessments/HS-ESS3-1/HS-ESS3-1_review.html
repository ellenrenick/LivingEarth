Key ideas to know

  1. Natural resources (fresh water, fertile soil, minerals, fossil fuels) are spread unevenly, and people settle where they can get them.
  2. Natural hazards (earthquakes, floods, volcanoes, hurricanes, droughts) can harm people, but many people live in hazardous places because the benefits are large.
  3. Some features are both a resource and a hazard. A flood plain has fertile soil but floods.
  4. People adapt to hazards, for example with earthquake-resistant buildings, levees, and early warnings.
  5. Changes in climate (less rain, rising seas) can change what farmers can grow and push people to move.
  6. Use data from maps and tables to connect a resource, hazard, or climate factor to what people do.
