## LS1-6.1 (DOK 1, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can name the three elements that make up most of sugar and the body's large molecules.

**Q1.** Which three elements make up a sugar such as glucose?

A. Calcium, carbon, and oxygen  
B. Carbon, nitrogen, and sulfur  
C. Hydrogen, oxygen, and iron  
D. Carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen  ✅  

*Answer D.* Sugar is made only of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen.

**Q2.** Amino acids contain carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen. What else do all amino acids contain?

A. Nitrogen  ✅  
B. Iron  
C. Helium  
D. Calcium  

*Answer A.* Amino acids also contain nitrogen (a few contain sulfur).

**Q3.** Which element is in an amino acid but is **not** in sugar?

A. Oxygen  
B. Nitrogen  ✅  
C. Hydrogen  
D. Carbon  

*Answer B.* Sugar has no nitrogen, so the nitrogen in amino acids has to come from somewhere else.

## LS1-6.2 (DOK 2, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can describe how living things take in matter and rearrange its atoms, with no atoms lost.

**Q1.** A student builds a molecule from beads, takes it apart, and builds a different molecule with the same beads. What does this show?

A. Atoms disappear when a molecule is taken apart.  
B. Atoms change into other elements.  
C. New atoms form when a molecule is built.  
D. Atoms are rearranged, and none are created or destroyed.  ✅  

*Answer D.* Matter is conserved: atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed.

**Q2.** A plant takes in CO2 and water and builds sugar. Compared with the atoms taken in, the atoms in the sugar are

A. the same atoms, rearranged into a new molecule  ✅  
B. new atoms made by the plant  
C. atoms of different elements  
D. fewer atoms, because some are lost  

*Answer A.* The carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen atoms are rearranged into sugar.

**Q3.** In 1 or 2 sentences, explain what happens to the atoms in food when your body uses it to build new body parts.

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Says the atoms are taken in and rearranged into new molecules, with none lost or created. |
| 1 | Says the food is used or broken down, but does not say the atoms are rearranged or conserved. |
| 0 | Blank or says atoms disappear or are created. |

*Model answer:* The atoms in food are rearranged into the molecules of new body parts. No atoms are lost or made, only moved into new arrangements.

## LS1-6.3 (DOK 2, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can explain how atoms from sugar join with other elements, like nitrogen, to build larger molecules.

**Q1.** A plant has plenty of sugar but no nitrogen in the soil. Why can it not build amino acids?

A. Plants do not make amino acids.  
B. Amino acids are made of sugar only.  
C. Sugar blocks the plant from making amino acids.  
D. Sugar has no nitrogen, and amino acids need nitrogen.  ✅  

*Answer D.* Atoms from sugar must join with nitrogen from another source to build amino acids.

**Q2.** Which statement is correct?

A. Atoms from sugar are rearranged and combined with other elements, such as nitrogen, to build larger molecules.  ✅  
B. Larger molecules are made by creating new atoms.  
C. Nitrogen atoms turn into carbon atoms.  
D. Sugar becomes amino acids without any other atoms.  

*Answer A.* Building amino acids takes atoms from sugar plus atoms of other elements.

**Q3.** Where does the nitrogen come from that a plant combines with atoms from sugar? Explain in 1 or 2 sentences how it is used.

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Names a source (soil or fertilizer) and says the nitrogen joins with atoms from sugar to build amino acids. |
| 1 | Names the source or the use, but not both. |
| 0 | Blank or incorrect. |

*Model answer:* The nitrogen comes from the soil. The plant combines it with carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen atoms from sugar to build amino acids.

## LS1-6.4 (DOK 2, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can use a model to trace atoms from sugar into a larger molecule.

**Q1.** In a bead model, black beads are carbon, white beads are hydrogen, red beads are oxygen, and blue beads are nitrogen. A student wants to build an amino acid from sugar beads. What must she add?

A. A different kind of sugar  
B. More black beads made from nothing  
C. Nothing; sugar has everything  
D. Blue (nitrogen) beads from outside the sugar  ✅  

*Answer D.* Sugar has no nitrogen, so nitrogen beads have to be added.

**Q2.** The student builds the amino acid and has some red and white beads left over. What does this show?

A. Not every atom from the sugar ends up in the amino acid; the leftover atoms are still there and can be used elsewhere.  ✅  
B. The leftover beads became nitrogen.  
C. The leftover atoms disappeared.  
D. The model is wrong because all atoms must be used.  

*Answer A.* Atoms are conserved, so leftover atoms form other molecules.

**Q3.** Use the bead model idea to trace the atoms: start with a sugar and an amino acid you build from it. Say which atoms come from the sugar and which are added.

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen are traced from the sugar into the amino acid and nitrogen is identified as added. |
| 1 | Traces atoms from sugar but does not identify what is added, or the reverse. |
| 0 | Blank or incorrect. |

*Model answer:* Carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen atoms from the sugar are rearranged into the amino acid. Nitrogen is added from outside because sugar has none.

## LS1-6.5 (DOK 3, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can use evidence to explain how atoms from sugar combine with other elements to build large carbon-based molecules.

**Q1.** | Plant | Nitrogen added | Sugar (mg/g) | Protein (mg/g) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Yes | 14 | 40 |
| B | No | 30 | 8 |

Which conclusion is best supported by the data?

A. Plant B could not make sugar.  
B. Plant A made less sugar because it was not getting light.  
C. Nitrogen is a kind of sugar.  
D. Plant B made sugar but without nitrogen could not build much protein.  ✅  

*Answer D.* Both plants made sugar; only the plant with nitrogen built much protein.

**Q2.** Which piece of evidence supports the claim that atoms from sugar are used to build proteins?

A. Labeled carbon from CO<sub>2</sub> shows up first in sugar and later in proteins.  ✅  
B. Plants are green.  
C. Plants need water.  
D. Proteins are larger than sugars.  

*Answer A.* A tracer moving from sugar to proteins shows the atoms were reused.

**Q3.** Using the table above, write 2 or 3 sentences that use data to explain why Plant B grows poorly even though it has more sugar.

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Uses data (more sugar, less protein) and explains that without nitrogen the sugar's atoms cannot be built into proteins. |
| 1 | Uses data or gives the idea, but not both. |
| 0 | Blank or claims plants cannot make sugar without nitrogen. |

*Model answer:* Plant B has more sugar (30 vs. 14 mg/g) but much less protein (8 vs. 40 mg/g). Without nitrogen it cannot combine atoms from sugar to build amino acids and proteins, so it cannot grow much.

## LS1-6.6 (DOK 3, 6 points)

**Learning target:** I can revise my explanation when new evidence comes in.

**Q1.** A student wrote, “Plants make all their molecules from sunlight.” New evidence shows labeled carbon from CO2 ends up in plant proteins. Which revision is best?

A. No revision is needed.  
B. Plants make molecules from soil only.  
C. Plants change sunlight into carbon.  
D. Plants use light energy to build sugar from CO2 and water, and atoms from that sugar are used to build other molecules.  ✅  

*Answer D.* Light supplies energy; the atoms come from CO2, water, and soil.

**Q2.** Why should a scientist revise an explanation when new evidence does not fit it?

A. Because an explanation should match all the evidence we have.  ✅  
B. Because explanations can never be right.  
C. Because the first explanation is always the best.  
D. Because evidence does not matter.  

*Answer A.* Scientific explanations are revised to fit new evidence.

**Q3.** Your first explanation was, “Plants get all the matter they need from the soil.” Then you learn that the soil in a pot barely changes in mass while the plant grows a lot. Revise your explanation in 1 or 2 sentences.

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Revised explanation uses the new evidence and says most of the matter comes from CO2 and water, with small amounts from the soil. |
| 1 | Revises, but does not use the evidence or does not name the new source. |
| 0 | Blank or repeats the original claim. |

*Model answer:* Because the soil barely changed, most of the plant's matter must come from CO2 in the air and water. The soil supplies small amounts of elements such as nitrogen.
