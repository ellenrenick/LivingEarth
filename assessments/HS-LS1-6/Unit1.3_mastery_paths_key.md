## CFA reviews ("Show what you know")

**LS1-6.1: Elements in sugar and large molecules**
1. Carbon, hydrogen, oxygen.
2. Nitrogen (or sulfur). A plant gets it from the soil, for example nitrate.
3. 45 + 42 + 6 = 93% of the dry pumpkin is C, O, and H, the same elements as sugar. Only about 3% is nitrogen.

**LS1-6.2: Taking in and rearranging matter**
1. From the food it eats. It breaks the food molecules down and rearranges the atoms into its own molecules.
2. C: 6 / 6. H: 12 / 12. O: 18 / 18 (12 + 6 on the left; 6 + 12 on the right).
3. Atoms are only rearranged, never created or destroyed. Any atoms that leave the new molecule, such as in a released water molecule, are still there, so the total mass is the same.

**LS1-6.3: Sugar atoms plus other elements**
1. The plant rearranges carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen atoms from sugar and combines them with nitrogen to make an amino acid.
2. Nitrogen, from nitrate in the soil.
3. Photosynthesis doesn't need nitrogen, so the corn still makes sugar. Without nitrogen it can't build amino acids and proteins for new cells, so it stays small.

**LS1-6.4: Trace atoms with a model**
1. CO2 in the air → sugar made by grass (photosynthesis) → grass amino acids, proteins, and other molecules (with nitrogen) → eaten and broken down by the cow → rebuilt into cow muscle protein.
2. 2 glycine = 4 C, 10 H, 2 N, 4 O. Left over: 2 black, 2 white, 2 red. Must add: 2 blue.
3. The labeled carbon appears first in sugar and later in amino acids and proteins, so the atoms in proteins passed through sugar.

**LS1-6.5: Explain with evidence (bean CER)**
1. Claim: the no-nitrogen plants were smaller because, without nitrogen, they couldn't turn their sugar into amino acids and proteins for new cells.
2. Evidence: protein was 9 vs. 35 mg/g, dry mass was 2.1 vs. 6.0 g, and sugar was higher (27 vs. 14 mg/g).
3. Reasoning: sugar contains only C, H, and O. Amino acids need nitrogen too, and atoms can't be created, so the sugar piled up unused.

**LS1-6.6: Revise an explanation (fish)**
1. Supported: the atoms in the fish's protein came from the algae (tracer).
2. Not supported: "unchanged." The fish protein's amino acid order is different from any algae protein.
3. Revised: algae take in CO2 and build sugar, then combine atoms from the sugar with nitrogen to make amino acids and proteins. The fish eats the algae, breaks these molecules down, and rearranges the atoms into its own proteins in a new order.

## Practice test review: Back to the Building Blocks
1. Glucose: yes / no. Glycine: yes / yes.
2. Left: 6 × 2 + 6 × 1 = 18. Right: 6 + 6 × 2 = 18.
3. carbon, hydrogen, oxygen; nitrogen; the soil (or food, for animals).
4. B, D, E, A, C.
5. The claim is that missing nitrogen kept the lettuce from turning sugar into protein. The evidence is protein 7 vs. 38 mg/g, mass 95 vs. 310 g, and sugar higher at 28 vs. 15 mg/g. The reasoning is that sugar has only C, H, and O, and proteins need nitrogen.
6. Fertilizer isn't food or an energy source. It supplies elements like nitrogen and phosphorus. Plants use light energy to make their own sugar, then combine atoms from the sugar with those elements to build amino acids, proteins, and other molecules.

## Extension: Algae for Fuel or Food?
1. CO2 → algae sugar (photosynthesis) → (a) oil: sugar's C, H, and O rearranged, no nitrogen needed; (b) protein: atoms from sugar plus nitrogen → amino acids → protein. Nitrogen is needed only on the protein path.
2. Tank A: protein 2.0 × 0.50 = 1.0 g/L; oil 2.0 × 0.15 = 0.30 g/L. Tank B: protein 1.0 × 0.15 = 0.15 g/L; oil 1.0 × 0.45 = 0.45 g/L. Oil is almost all C, H, and O, which sugar supplies, but protein needs nitrogen.
3. The CER should explain that Tank B lacked nitrogen, so it couldn't build the amino acids and proteins that new cells need. It should cite 1.0 vs. 2.0 g/L of dry mass and 15% vs. 50% protein, and connect sugar's C, H, and O plus nitrogen to amino acids.
4. Tank B made more oil per liter (0.45 vs. 0.30 g/L), so the claim is partly supported for fuel. But Tank B grew half as much and made very little protein. A good revision says it depends on the goal: starve the algae of nitrogen for fuel (perhaps after growing them with nitrogen first), but keep nitrogen for food, and weigh the cost of fertilizer.
