Key ideas to know

  1. Sugar is made of only carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen. Plants build sugar (glucose) from carbon dioxide and water during photosynthesis. Light supplies the energy. CO2 and water supply the atoms.
  2. Sugar is the starting material for other molecules. Cells rearrange the C, H, and O atoms from sugar and combine them with other elements, such as nitrogen, sulfur, and phosphorus, to build amino acids and other large carbon-based molecules. Plants get these other elements from the soil or water.
  3. Small molecules link into big ones. Amino acids (monomers) link together in a specific order to make proteins (polymers).
  4. Atoms are conserved. Atoms are rearranged. They are never created, destroyed, or changed into a different element. If a molecule has nitrogen and sugar doesn't, the nitrogen had to come from somewhere else.
  5. Matter moves through food chains. When an animal eats, it breaks food molecules down into smaller pieces and rebuilds those atoms into its own molecules. It does not store the food's proteins unchanged.
