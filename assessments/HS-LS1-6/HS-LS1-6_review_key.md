# HS-LS1-6 Review: Answer Key

Use with `HS-LS1-6_review.html`. Students do it on the review day, after the CFAs and before the CSA. Plan on 30 to 40 minutes. The Vocabulary and Write It sections are for everyone; students pick the other sections by their CFA scores (below 4 of 6).

## Vocabulary

1. photosynthesis
2. glucose
3. element; atom
4. amino acid
5. conservation of matter

## Section A: Elements in sugar and other molecules

1. Carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen.
2. Serine has nitrogen. Methionine has nitrogen and sulfur.
3. From the soil (taken in by the roots), including from fertilizer.

## Section B: Atoms are conserved

1. The number of each color stays the same. Beads (atoms) are rearranged and none are created or lost.
2. Atoms are conserved and cannot change from one element to another. Nitrogen has to come from an outside source.
3. False. Most comes from CO<sub>2</sub> in the air and water, rearranged into sugar and other molecules. The soil supplies small amounts of elements like nitrogen.

## Section C: Combining atoms from sugar with other elements

1. Reuse black, white, and red (C, H, O). Add blue (nitrogen) beads, because glucose has no nitrogen.
2. Not every atom from sugar goes into the amino acid. The leftover atoms are conserved and can be used in other molecules.
3. CO<sub>2</sub> in the air, built into sugar by the clover (photosynthesis), rearranged with nitrogen into the clover's amino acids and proteins, eaten by the rabbit, broken down, and rebuilt into the rabbit's own proteins.

## Section D: Using evidence and revising

1. Group B made more sugar (30 vs. 14 mg/g) but much less protein (8 vs. 40 mg/g).
2. Without nitrogen, Group B cannot combine atoms from sugar with nitrogen to build amino acids and proteins, so sugar builds up.
3. Plants use light energy to build sugar from CO<sub>2</sub> and water; atoms from that sugar are rearranged and combined with elements like nitrogen from the soil to build proteins. Light is energy, not matter.
4. Group B has high sugar, so it still does photosynthesis. Nitrogen is needed to build proteins, not to make sugar.

## Write It

**Checklist (what a strong answer includes):**

- States that the lack of sulfur limited building proteins even though sugar was made
- Uses at least two numbers (sugar and protein or yield)
- Says sugar has only C, H, and O, and sulfur has to come from the soil
- Says atoms from sugar are rearranged and combined with sulfur (and other elements) to build the molecules for growth
- Explains why sugar built up and predicts sugar drops and protein and yield rise after adding sulfur

**Model answer:** Field 2 grew less because it lacked sulfur, so it could not turn its sugar into the proteins needed to grow. Field 2 had more sugar (27 vs. 16 mg/g) but less protein (6% vs. 13%) and a lower yield (3.1 vs. 7.5 tons per hectare). Sugar contains only carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen. To build some amino acids, the plant combines atoms from sugar with sulfur and nitrogen from the soil. Without sulfur, Field 2's sugar could not be used, so it built up. If sulfur were added, the sugar would be used to build amino acids and proteins, so sugar would drop and protein and yield would rise.
