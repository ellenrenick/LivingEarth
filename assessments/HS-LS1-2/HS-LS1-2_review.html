Key ideas to know

  1. Cells are the basic unit of life. Every living thing is made of one or more cells. A bacterium is one cell; a human has trillions.
  2. Levels of organization, smallest to largest: cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism. Each level is built from the level below it.
  3. A tissue is a group of similar cells doing one job. An organ is made of several different tissues working together. An organ system is a group of organs working together.
  4. Structure fits function. The shape and makeup of a part helps it do its job, like the many folds of the small intestine or the thin walls of the air sacs in the lungs.
  5. Systems depend on each other. Movement needs the nervous, muscular, and skeletal systems. Delivering oxygen needs the respiratory and circulatory systems.
  6. A change at one level affects the levels above it. If a tissue in an artery is damaged, the artery, the circulatory system, and the organs it serves are affected.

Section A: Levels and cells

Use the levels of organization and the idea that all living things are made of cells.

  1. Put these in order from smallest to largest: organ system, cell, organism, tissue, organ.
  2. Name the level of organization for each: (a) a sheet of similar lining cells in the stomach, (b) the stomach, (c) the digestive system, (d) a single bacterium.
  3. True or false: Every living thing has organs. Explain.
  4. Why is the cell called the basic unit of life?

Section B: Building up from the level below

Each level is built from the level below it.

  1. Complete the chain for the muscular system: muscle cell → ____________ → biceps (an ____________) → ____________ → human (an ____________).
  2. The heart has muscle tissue, connective tissue, and nervous tissue. Why is the heart an organ and not a tissue?
  3. A student says, “A tissue is made of organs.” Correct the statement.

Section D: Using and building models

A model shows parts and how they connect, with arrows. A model of levels shows each level built from the one below.

  1. Make a flow model with arrows for catching a ball. Include the nervous, muscular, and skeletal systems.
  2. A student's model is: brain → leg muscles → legs move. What is missing, and why does it matter?
  3. Plan your own model of how the body digests food and delivers it to the cells. List three parts and the arrows between them.
  4. Add the levels of organization to your model: name a cell, a tissue, an organ, and the system for one part.
