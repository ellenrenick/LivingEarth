# HS-LS1-2 Review: Answer Key

Use with `HS-LS1-2_review.html`. Students do it on the review day, after the CFAs and before the CSA. Plan on 30 to 40 minutes. The Vocabulary and Write It sections are for everyone; students pick the other sections by their CFA scores (below 4 of 6).

## Vocabulary

1. cell
2. tissue
3. organ
4. organ system
5. organism
6. structure and function

## Section A: Levels and cells

1. Cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism.
2. (a) tissue, (b) organ, (c) organ system, (d) organism (a one-celled organism).
3. False. Many organisms, like bacteria, are one cell with no tissues or organs. Every living thing is made of at least one cell.
4. It is the smallest unit that can carry out the functions of life, and all living things are made of cells.

## Section B: Building up from the level below

1. Muscle cell, muscle tissue, biceps (organ), muscular system, human (organism).
2. An organ is made of several different tissues that work together to do one job. The heart has three kinds of tissue, so it is an organ.
3. A tissue is made of cells. Organs are made of tissues.

## Section C: Structure, function, and working together

1. Folds give the intestine a large surface to take in digested food. Thin walls let oxygen pass quickly into the blood. Root hairs give a large surface for taking in water. Long nerve cells carry signals quickly over long distances.
2. The respiratory system takes in more oxygen, and the circulatory system moves blood faster to carry that oxygen to the working muscles.
3. The muscles get less oxygen and tire quickly, because the circulatory system delivers oxygen to them. One system's failure affects the others.
4. The artery (organ) cannot regulate blood flow well, so the circulatory system delivers blood less effectively, and the organs that depend on it work poorly, which affects the whole person.

## Section D: Using and building models

1. Eyes see ball and nerves send signals (nervous system) &rarr; brain sends signals to arm muscles (nervous to muscular) &rarr; muscles contract and pull on bones (muscular and skeletal) &rarr; hand closes on ball.
2. The skeletal system. Muscles move the body by pulling on bones, so a complete model needs the bones.
3. Example: stomach and small intestine (digestive system) break down food and pass nutrients into the blood &rarr; circulatory system carries them &rarr; cells of the body use them. Any three real parts with correct arrows are acceptable.
4. Example: small intestine lining cell, lining tissue, small intestine (organ), digestive system.

## Write It

**Checklist (what a strong answer includes):**

- States a claim that damage to one organ system affects other systems and the whole person
- Uses at least two numbers from the table
- Names at least three organ systems and what each does
- Shows how the systems depend on each other (arrows or sentences)
- Explains one structure-function fit (for example, thin walls of the air sacs)

**Model answer:** The damaged lungs lower the oxygen that reaches the body, which makes the heart and muscles work harder and tire sooner. Only 60% of the normal oxygen reaches the blood, the heart rate on the stairs rises from 110 to 150, and the leg muscles tire after 3 minutes instead of 12. The respiratory system takes in oxygen, the circulatory system carries it to the muscular system, and the heart beats faster to make up for the lower oxygen. The air sacs of healthy lungs have thin walls that let oxygen pass quickly into the blood, but damaged air sacs cannot, so the whole chain suffers.
